Best Website Builders in Malaysia (2026): An Honest Comparison

Blake Ng
Co-founder, Kong Last updated: June 2026 16 min read

Why you can trust this comparison

I've spent years helping Malaysian tour and small-business operators get online — and I co-run a website-and-booking platform called Kong, which is on this list. I'm telling you that in the first paragraph because the rest of this article only works if you trust I'm being straight with you.

So here's my promise: I'll tell you where each builder genuinely wins, where it genuinely struggles, and — importantly — where Kong is the wrong choice. Kong is the right answer for exactly one kind of business (booking businesses), and for most of the businesses reading this, one of the other builders is the better pick. I'd rather tell you that honestly than lose your trust by pretending Kong is best at everything. I've watched hundreds of operators pick the wrong tool, sink weeks into it, and give up — usually because a listicle told them to use Shopify when they needed something completely different. This guide is the version I wish they'd read first.

If you just want the answer, skip to the recommendation below.

TL;DR — which builder should you actually use?

One-line recommendations by business type:

Pick by what you sell

  • Selling physical products to Malaysians? → SiteGiant (local, FPX and e-wallets built in, syncs with Shopee/Lazada/TikTok) or Shopify (global scale, but add a local payment gateway).
  • Want a beautiful general business or brand site? → Wix (easiest and most flexible) or Squarespace (most polished design).
  • On the tightest budget, or want AI to build it for you? → An AI builder like Hostinger, from around RM 10/month.
  • Need full control, a real blog, and no vendor lock-in? → WordPress, if you're willing to maintain it (or pay someone to).
  • Running a booking business — tours, activities, classes, rentals? → A booking-native builder like Kong. Free for the business, with FPX/e-wallet support via Xendit and bookings built in. This is the one case where I'll tell you Kong is the clear right answer — because none of the others are built for selling by the session.

The honest, detailed version is below. Pick what fits your business — there's no universal winner.

How to choose a website builder: the criteria that matter

Before comparing brands, get clear on what your business actually needs. These are the dimensions the choice really turns on — in rough order of how much they should weigh on your decision:

  1. Products or bookings? This is the biggest fork, and most guides skip it. A website that sells physical products (cart, inventory, shipping) is a fundamentally different tool from one that takes bookings for a time-slot (capacity per session, dates, deposits). Most builders are built for the first; very few do the second properly. Decide which you are before anything else.
  2. Local Malaysian payments. If your buyers are Malaysian, they expect FPX online banking, Touch 'n Go, GrabPay, ShopeePay and DuitNow QR at checkout — not just credit cards. Global builders (Wix, Squarespace, Shopify) don't support these natively; you bolt on a gateway like HitPay. Local platforms (SiteGiant, Kong) include them. Missing the payment methods your customers use is the quietest way to lose sales.
  3. Ease of use vs. your time. AI and drag-and-drop builders get you live fastest; WordPress is the most capable but needs real setup and upkeep. Be honest about how much time you'll actually put in.
  4. Scalability and vendor lock-in. Some builders (Wix, Shopify, Squarespace) are "closed" — you can't export your site, so outgrowing or leaving them means rebuilding from scratch. WordPress and self-hosted options let you move freely. If you expect to grow or switch, lock-in is a real long-term cost, not a footnote.
  5. Builder vs. hosting — they're not the same thing. A website builder (Wix, Squarespace, Kong) bundles the design tools and the hosting, so your site goes live automatically. Hosting alone (or WordPress) means you assemble the pieces. For most small businesses, a bundled builder is simpler; you only need to think about separate hosting if you're going the WordPress route.

Hold your answers to these in mind as you read the builders below — they'll point you straight to your shortlist. And keep the finish line in view: in our 2026 study of 252 Malaysian operators, among operators that do have a live website, 70% still cannot take a booking on it — the site takes enquiries only (Kong, 2026, n=66). Building the site is only half the job; it has to take the booking.

The real choice: what are you actually selling?

Most "best website builder" lists pretend every builder competes with every other one. They don't. Once you cut through it, you're really choosing between three groups, and which group you're in matters far more than which brand you pick within it:

  • Selling physical products? Your real shortlist is SiteGiant, Shopify, or WordPress+WooCommerce — tools built around carts, inventory and shipping.
  • Just need a professional presence (brochure, brand, portfolio)? Your shortlist is Wix, Squarespace, or an AI builder like Hostinger — tools built around pages and design.
  • Selling bookings — by the session, not the box? Your shortlist is a booking-native tool like Kong — built around capacity, dates and deposits, which the other two groups genuinely can't do well.

Figure out your group first. It turns an overwhelming list of "the 10 best builders" into a two-or-three-way choice you can actually make.

The builders, one by one

Wix — best for a beautiful general business site

The most popular general-purpose builder in the world, and the easiest way to get a genuinely good-looking site live fast — but you pay in US dollars, need a mid-tier plan to take payments, and can never move your site off Wix.

Pricing (2026): Free plan (with Wix ads + a Wix subdomain — testing only). Paid: Light ~USD 17/mo, Core ~USD 29/mo, Business ~USD 39/mo, Business Elite ~USD 159/mo (~RM 75–700). You need Core (~USD 29) to take payments — Light won't sell. No Wix transaction fee, but ~2.9% + USD 0.30 gateway fee applies.Pricing per Wix, verified June 2026.

Where Wix genuinely wins: the drag-and-drop editor is the most beginner-friendly here, with over 900 templates (the biggest library in this list) and a standout mobile editor that lets you tune the phone version separately — which matters in mobile-first Malaysia. Its SEO tools are strong, and in 2026 it added AI site generation ("describe it, it builds it"). For a café, service business, portfolio or brochure site, it's hard to go wrong.

Where Wix genuinely struggles: the big one is vendor lock-in — you can't export your site, so if you outgrow Wix or want to leave, you rebuild from scratch. It's also priced in USD (your bill moves with the exchange rate), and it doesn't support FPX or Touch 'n Go natively — you add a gateway like HitPay.

Best for: a general business, café, or creative who wants a polished site live this week with minimal fuss. Worst for: anyone who expects to outgrow it, or who needs native local payments without a workaround.

Squarespace — best for design-led brand sites

The most beautiful output of any builder here, with the least effort — but there's no free plan, the cheapest selling tier takes a commission, and "Squarespace does bookings" really means "pay for a second product."

Pricing (2026): No free plan (14-day trial). Roughly Basic ~USD 16–25/mo, Core ~USD 23–33/mo, Plus ~USD 39–65/mo, Advanced ~USD 99–139/mo (~RM 70–625), varying by region and billing. Basic allows selling but takes a 2–3% commission; Core drops that to 0%.Pricing per Squarespace, verified June 2026.

Where Squarespace genuinely wins: around 180 curated, polished templates that are genuinely hard to make look bad, and a structured editor that prevents the design mistakes beginners make on Wix's free-form canvas. For a brand, creative, or service business where first impressions are everything, it produces the classiest result here.

Where Squarespace genuinely struggles: the booking catch — appointment booking runs through Acuity Scheduling, a separate product at ~USD 16–61/mo, so booking isn't really "included." Design control is more constrained than Wix, there's no free plan, and like Wix it has no native Malaysian payments and the same lock-in problem.

Best for: a design-first brand or service business that wants to look premium and doesn't need local payments or serious booking tools. Worst for: a booking business, or anyone on a tight budget (no free tier, commission on the cheap plan).

Shopify — best for serious physical-product ecommerce

The global standard for selling physical products at volume — but for a booking business it's the wrong tool entirely, and in Malaysia its own payment system doesn't even work.

Pricing (2026): Basic ~USD 39/mo, Shopify ~USD 105/mo, Advanced ~USD 399/mo (~RM 175–1,800). Crucially, Shopify Payments isn't available in Malaysia, so you route payments through a local gateway like iPay88 or HitPay — and Shopify adds a 2% fee on external gateways on the Basic plan on top.Pricing per Shopify; Malaysia payment limitation verified June 2026.

Where Shopify genuinely wins: the deepest ecommerce toolset here by far — inventory, variants, shipping rules, multichannel selling — backed by an 8,000+ app store for anything it doesn't do natively. If you're selling hundreds of products or scaling across channels, nothing else in this list competes.

Where Shopify genuinely struggles: it's the most expensive option once you add the apps most stores end up needing; Shopify Payments not working in Malaysia means an extra gateway and the 2% surcharge; and it has the same vendor lock-in as Wix. It's also overkill — and miscast — for any business that isn't selling physical products.

Best for: a growing Malaysian product store, especially one selling across multiple channels. Worst for: booking businesses, and small/simple sites that would drown in features they'll never use.

SiteGiant — best for Malaysian multichannel ecommerce

The strongest local option for product sellers — native FPX and e-wallets, and one dashboard for your own store plus Shopee, Lazada and TikTok. Built for Malaysia in a way the global players aren't, though it's still a product-cart system, not a booking tool.

Pricing: SiteGiant sells on yearly plans rather than a flat public monthly fee (Webstore Mini/Basic/Pro, scaling by order volume, SKUs and sales channels), with no transaction fee on your own webstore sales.SiteGiant doesn't publish a flat monthly price; confirm current plans directly.

Where SiteGiant genuinely wins: the local fit is real and hard to match — native FPX and e-wallet support, 70+ templates, and built-in syncing with Shopee, Lazada and TikTok so you run your webstore and marketplace listings from one inventory. It even includes a POS for bridging online and offline sales. For a Malaysian seller living across marketplaces, nothing else here is as purpose-fit.

Where SiteGiant genuinely struggles: some users report the webstore can be slower than the global builders, and the deeper ERP/inventory features have a real learning curve. And like everything else in this section, it's built for products — carts, SKUs, shipping — not for selling by the session.

Best for: a Malaysian product seller who lives on Shopee/Lazada/TikTok and wants their own branded store too. Worst for: booking businesses, and anyone wanting a simple brochure site (it's more machine than you'd need).

Hostinger — best for the cheapest simple or AI-built site

The cheapest credible way to get a simple site online, with AI that builds you a starter in under a minute. Deliberately basic — fine for a brochure or portfolio, not for anything that needs to scale or get custom.

Pricing (2026): from around USD 2–3/month (~RM 10–13) on longer commitments, including a free domain, SSL, ~150 templates, AI site generation, and basic ecommerce (up to ~500 products). Renewal rates rise after the first term.Pricing per Hostinger, verified June 2026.

Where Hostinger genuinely wins: price and speed. Nothing else here is this cheap, and the AI builder generates a complete starter site (layout, copy, images) from a few prompts in under a minute. For a simple site you want live today on a tiny budget, it's excellent value.

Where Hostinger genuinely struggles: it's template-based and intentionally simple — fewer design blocks, a thin app/integration set (you paste code snippets rather than install apps), no native Malaysian payments, and renewal pricing that jumps. It won't grow into a complex store or a custom build.

Best for: a simple brochure or portfolio site, fast and cheap, especially via AI. Worst for: anyone who'll need real scalability, deep customisation, or native local payments. (More on AI builders in the website cost guide.)

WordPress — best for full control and content

The most powerful and flexible option, the best for blogging and content-SEO, and the only one with zero lock-in — you own everything and can move it anywhere. The price is effort: you (or a developer) build and maintain it.

Pricing: typically ~RM 2,000–3,000 for a developer to build it, plus ongoing hosting, plugins, security and updates you manage yourself. The software itself (WordPress.org) is free.Typical Malaysian developer setup cost, 2026.

Where WordPress genuinely wins: complete control and ownership. It has the best blogging and content-SEO capability in this list — genuinely important if ranking on Google is part of your plan — and no vendor lock-in (unlike Wix, Squarespace and Shopify, you can move your whole site freely). Anything is possible with the right plugins.

Where WordPress genuinely struggles: effort and upkeep. You're responsible for hosting, updates, security and backups, and bookings or a store come via plugins (WooCommerce plus a booking add-on) — more moving parts to keep working. It's the steepest learning curve here.

Best for: a business that wants control and serious content, and has technical comfort or a developer on call. Worst for: a non-technical owner who just wants to be online this week without managing anything.

Kong — best for booking businesses (tours, activities, classes, rentals)

The one builder here made for businesses sold by the session rather than the product — free for the business, with native Malaysian payments and bookings built in. The honest flip side: it's a booking site, not a custom-branded brand site or a blog.

Pricing: Free for the business — no setup fee, no monthly fee. Guests pay a 1.8% fee at checkout on online bookings only (walk-in, phone and WhatsApp bookings are free). You connect your own Stripe or Xendit account.Kong pricing per bookwithkong.com.

Where Kong genuinely wins: it's built for the thing the other six can't do — selling by the session: capacity per session, dates and time-slots, deposits, and no-oversell logic, with automatic confirmations. Via Xendit it brings the local payment methods Malaysian guests actually use — FPX, Touch 'n Go, ShopeePay and DuitNow QR — and it's genuinely free for the operator. You get a booking page plus unlimited additional pages built from modules (text, image galleries, hero image, product grids, bookable products).

Where Kong genuinely struggles — and I'll be straight about this: you get a Kong web address (yourbusiness.bookwithkong.com), not your own custom domain. It's not a blog or content-SEO platform, and it's not a physical-product store — no cart, inventory or shipping. If you need a bespoke-designed brand site, a content/blog engine, or to sell physical products, one of the other builders above is the better choice, and I'd genuinely point you there.

Best for: any business that sells experiences or time-slots — tour operators, dive centres, cooking schools, ATV and kayak rentals, escape rooms, workshop and class hosts. Worst for: physical-product stores, content-heavy brand sites, or anyone who needs a custom domain as a non-negotiable.

Why a booking business needs its own kind of builder

I want to expand on the booking-business case, because it's the one every other guide gets wrong. A huge share of Malaysian businesses don't sell products at all: tour operators, dive centres, cooking schools, ATV and kayak rentals, escape rooms, workshop hosts, fitness studios. These are booking businesses, and they have genuinely different needs from a product store:

  • Capacity per session, not stock levels — you're selling 12 seats on the 9am departure, not 200 units in a warehouse.
  • Dates and time-slots, not a product catalogue — guests pick when, not just what.
  • Deposits and full payment, not shipping — there's nothing to post; the "delivery" is the experience.
  • No-oversell protection — once the boat is full, it's full, and the system has to stop selling that slot.

Putting a booking business on Shopify or SiteGiant means forcing a square peg into a round hole — bending a product-cart system to fake time-slots, which is painful and usually ends with the operator giving up and going back to taking bookings over WhatsApp. I've watched it happen many times. That's the gap a booking-native builder fills. (We go deeper on this in our guide to building a website for your tour business.)

What about free website builders?

"Free website builder Malaysia" is a common search, and the honest answer is: free options exist, but read the limits before you commit. Most free tiers (like Wix's) put ads on your site, give you a builder-branded subdomain rather than your own domain, and don't let you take payments — so for a real business, "free" usually becomes a paid plan the moment you want to actually sell.

The exception worth knowing: a couple of platforms are genuinely free for the business and make their money on transaction fees instead of a subscription. Kong is one — free for the operator, with guests paying 1.8% only on online bookings — which works out free to set up and use for a booking business. For a product store, payment-platform builders like HitPay's store work on a similar transaction-fee model. The rule of thumb: "free" with a monthly-fee builder means limited; "free" on a transaction-fee model means you pay only when you earn.

The honest recommendation

Match the tool to the business:

  • Selling physical products to Malaysians? SiteGiant (local, FPX built in, marketplace sync) or Shopify (global scale, add a local gateway).
  • Want a beautiful general business or brand site? Wix (easiest, most flexible) or Squarespace (most design-led).
  • On the tightest budget, or want AI to build it? An AI builder like Hostinger, from ~RM 10/month.
  • Want full control and serious content/blogging? WordPress, if you'll maintain it.
  • Running a booking business — tours, activities, classes, rentals? A booking-native builder like Kong: free, with local payments via Xendit and bookings built in.

There's no universally "best" builder — only the best one for your kind of business. Figure out whether you're selling products or bookings first, check it supports the payment methods your customers use, and the shortlist gets short fast.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best website builder in Malaysia?
There's no single best — it depends on your business. For physical-product ecommerce, SiteGiant (local, with FPX and marketplace sync) or Shopify lead. For a general business or brand site, Wix or Squarespace. For full control and content, WordPress. For a booking business like tours, activities or classes, a booking-native builder like Kong fits best because it handles sessions, dates and deposits rather than product carts.
What is the best free website builder in Malaysia?
Most "free" builder tiers (like Wix's) add ads, give you a branded subdomain, and don't let you take payments — so for a real business they usually become paid plans. The genuinely free options run on a transaction-fee model instead of a subscription: Kong is free for booking businesses (guests pay 1.8% only on online bookings), and HitPay's store works similarly for product sellers. You pay only when you earn rather than a monthly fee.
Which website builders support FPX and e-wallets in Malaysia?
Local platforms support them natively: SiteGiant for product ecommerce, and Kong for booking businesses (via Xendit — FPX, Touch 'n Go, ShopeePay, DuitNow QR). Global builders like Wix, Squarespace and Shopify don't support Malaysian payment methods on their own — you add a local payment gateway such as HitPay on top.
Is Shopify or SiteGiant better for a Malaysian online store?
Both are strong for physical-product ecommerce. SiteGiant is built for the Malaysian market with native FPX/e-wallet support and built-in Shopee, Lazada and TikTok syncing, which suits sellers working across marketplaces. Shopify offers larger global scale and a bigger app ecosystem but needs a local gateway added for Malaysian payment methods and is priced in USD. Neither is built for booking-based businesses.
What website builder should a tour or activity operator use?
Not a product-ecommerce platform. Tours, activities, classes and rentals are sold by the session, so they need capacity limits, dates and time-slots, and deposits — not a shopping cart and shipping. A booking-native builder like Kong is purpose-built for this, with bookings and local payments included, free for the operator. See our full guide to building a website for your tour business for the detail.
How much does a website builder cost in Malaysia?
It ranges widely. Global builders (Wix, Squarespace, Shopify) run roughly RM 75–175+ a month once you're on a plan that takes payments, priced in USD so it moves with the exchange rate. AI builders like Hostinger start from ~RM 10/month. A developer-built WordPress site is typically RM 2,000–3,000 to set up plus ongoing maintenance. Local SiteGiant is sold on yearly plans. Booking-native Kong is free for the operator, with guests paying 1.8% on online bookings only. For the full picture including agencies and freelancers, see our guide to how much a website costs in Malaysia.
Wix or Squarespace — which is better?
Both are excellent general builders. Wix is more flexible (free-form drag-and-drop, 900+ templates, a free plan, and AI site generation) and is cheaper the moment you start selling. Squarespace makes more polished-looking sites with less effort and is design-first, but has no free plan and takes a commission on its cheapest selling tier. Choose Wix for flexibility and value when selling; choose Squarespace when premium design is your top priority. Neither supports Malaysian payment methods natively, and neither is built for bookings.
What's the cheapest website builder in Malaysia?
AI builders are the cheapest credible option — Hostinger's builder starts around RM 10–13/month with a free domain and AI site generation. Wix and Squarespace start higher (~RM 70–130/month on a plan that takes payments). The genuinely free route is a transaction-fee model: Kong is free for booking businesses (guests pay 1.8% on online bookings only), and Wix's free tier exists but carries ads and a subdomain.
Can I move my website to another builder later?
Often not easily. Wix, Squarespace and Shopify are "closed" platforms — you can't export your site, so switching means rebuilding from scratch. This vendor lock-in is a real long-term consideration. WordPress is the most portable (you own and can move everything). If you think you'll switch or scale significantly, factor lock-in into your choice now.
What's the difference between a website builder and web hosting?
A website builder (Wix, Squarespace, Kong) bundles the design tools and the hosting together, so your site goes live automatically with nothing separate to buy. Web hosting alone just stores your site's files and serves them online — you'd pair it with something like WordPress to build the actual site. For most small businesses, a bundled builder is simpler; you only deal with separate hosting if you go the WordPress route.
