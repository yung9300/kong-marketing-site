MOTAC License: The Complete Guide for Malaysian Tour Operators (2026)

Blake Ng
Co-founder, Kong June 2026 12 min read

Last updated: June 2026

If you run tours or activities in Malaysia — ATV trails, jeep tours, cooking classes, island hopping, river cruises — and you want to operate legally and take bookings properly, you need a MOTAC licence. This guide walks you through exactly how to get one in 2026, in plain language, without the government-portal headache.

A quick honest note before we start: licensing rules and fees change, and some requirements vary depending on your licence type and ownership. We've kept this current as of 2026 and flagged where the numbers vary, but always confirm the latest details with MOTAC or a licensed company secretary before you act. This is a guide, not legal advice.

What is a MOTAC licence?

A MOTAC licence is the official licence Malaysian tour and travel businesses need to operate legally, issued by the Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture (MOTAC). The licence is formally called the TOBTAB licence — Tour Operating Business and Travel Agency Business — and it's what lets you legally sell and run tours in Malaysia.

It's worth some perspective on the licensed market you're joining. Per The Digital State of Malaysian Tour Operators 2026, Malaysia's official register lists 5,284 licensed travel agencies, but the ATV parks, dive centres and island boats visitors actually book are mostly not on it — and 74% of operators have no website of their own (Kong, 2026, n=252).

If you're running experiences for guests and taking payment for them, you fall under this licensing regime. It also comes with an ongoing obligation many operators miss — see our guide to the tourism tax in Malaysia. It applies whether you run a small ATV outfit in Selangor, jeep tours in the Cameron Highlands, or island trips out of Langkawi.

The short version: what you need

Here's the whole process in one glance. Each step is explained in full below.

  1. Register a company (Sdn Bhd) with SSM, with the right business activity wording.
  2. Secure commercial premises — a physical office in a commercial area, which MOTAC will inspect.
  3. Complete the TTMC course — at least one key person in your company must pass it, and your manager needs the right qualifications.
  4. Join a MOTAC-recognised tourism association.
  5. Apply through TOURLIST, MOTAC's online licensing portal, and pay the application fee.

Realistically, budget two to four months from start to licensed, and confirm the current capital requirements for your licence type directly with MOTAC — they vary widely (more on that below).

Which licence type do you need?

MOTAC licences come in a few scopes, and the one you need depends on what your business actually does. Most tour and activity operators running trips inside Malaysia need the inbound tour operating scope.

The main scopes are inbound tour operating (tours within Malaysia for visitors), outbound tour operating (sending travellers overseas), and ticketing (selling flight and transport tickets). If you run experiences — ATV, rafting, cooking classes, jeep tours, charters — inbound tour operating is almost certainly your starting point. You don't need the outbound or ticketing scopes unless you're packaging overseas travel or selling transport tickets, so don't pay for scopes you won't use. Confirm your exact scope with MOTAC, because it affects your requirements and your capital.

Step 1: Register your company (Sdn Bhd) with SSM

Before MOTAC will license you, you need a registered private limited company — a Sendirian Berhad (Sdn Bhd) — incorporated with the Companies Commission of Malaysia (SSM). A sole proprietorship or partnership will not qualify for a MOTAC tour licence.

Two specific things MOTAC checks at this stage:

  • Your company name must signal the business. It should clearly relate to tours or travel — names including words like "travel," "tours," "holiday," "vacation," or "adventure" are what MOTAC expects to see.
  • Your business activity wording must be exact. When you register with SSM, the business activity must specifically state that you will "carry business in Tour Operating Business and / or Travel Agency Business." Getting this wording right at registration saves you from having to amend it later.

If you're not comfortable with SSM incorporation, a licensed company secretary handles this routinely and it's the most common way operators get it done.

Step 2: Secure your premises

MOTAC requires a physical commercial premises, and they will inspect it before granting your licence. This is one of the steps operators most often underestimate.

The requirements are specific: the premises must be in a commercial area, not a residential one; it must have a permanent address; and it generally cannot be shared with an unrelated business. After you apply, MOTAC schedules a physical inspection — and because inspections are organised by state into zones, the wait can run anywhere from around two weeks to two months depending on where you are. Factor this into your timeline.

Step 3: Complete the TTMC course and meet the manager requirement

At least one key person in your company must complete the TTMC — the Travel and Tours Management Course — a MOTAC-accredited course that ends with an assessment. This is mandatory for new licence applications.

There are two people-related requirements to plan for:

  • The TTMC course: at least one key company representative — typically a director or the designated manager — must attend and pass it. It's commonly a two-day course costing around RM800 per person (confirm the current fee with the training provider). For licence renewals rather than new applications, the equivalent is the TTEC, the Travel and Tours Enhancement Course.
  • Manager qualification: your designated manager must either hold a tourism-related qualification (a relevant diploma or degree) or have a minimum of three years' experience in the tourism industry.

You'll also need to register your tour guides, drivers, and any tour vehicles you operate with MOTAC.

Step 4: Join a MOTAC-recognised association

Your company must be a member of at least one tourism association recognised by MOTAC. This is a genuine requirement, not an optional nicety.

Recognised associations include MATTA (Malaysian Association of Tour and Travel Agents), BUMITRA (Bumiputera Travel and Tour Agents Association of Malaysia), and MCTA (Malaysia Chinese Tourism Association), among others. Pick the one most relevant to your business; there's nothing stopping you joining more than one.

Step 5: Apply through TOURLIST and pay the fee

MOTAC has moved licensing online to a portal called TOURLIST, where you submit your application and documents. The application fee is RM100 per application, payable to the Commissioner of Tourism (verify the current fee when you apply).

You'll submit your company documents, proof of premises, your TTMC certificate, association membership, and details of your directors and shareholders. Once submitted, the premises inspection is scheduled, and assuming everything is in order, your licence follows.

How much does a MOTAC licence cost?

The MOTAC application fee itself is modest — RM100 per application — but the real cost of getting licensed is the paid-up capital you have to show and the setup around it. Here's the honest picture, with one important caveat: the capital requirement varies by the area you operate in and your licence scope, which is exactly why MOTAC itself doesn't publish a single flat number — its guidance says you need "sufficient paid-up capital according to the area where you wish to operate."

For a Malaysian-owned company, the figures most commonly cited as of 2026 fall in a range:

  • Paid-up capital: from around RM50,000 at the lower end, up to RM150,000–RM200,000 depending on your operating area and scope. As a rough guide, premises within a City Council area (like Dewan Bandaraya Kuala Lumpur) tend to sit at the higher end, while District Council areas can be lower. This is real money deposited into your company bank account — it becomes company funds you can use, and MOTAC checks your bank statement to confirm it's there.
  • Security deposit: around RM20,000, typically as a bank guarantee or bank draft, held by MOTAC as security against consumer complaints. This is separate from your paid-up capital.

For a foreign-owned company, the bar is much higher and tiered by ASEAN status and ownership share — commonly cited figures run from around RM500,000 (non-ASEAN, minority ownership) up to RM1,000,000 (ASEAN) and RM1,500,000 (100% foreign ownership). Foreign ownership of tour businesses is genuinely complex — work it through with a licensed company secretary.

Because these figures differ across sources and change over time, treat them as a planning range and confirm the exact current requirement for your area and licence type directly with MOTAC or a licensed company secretary before you commit money. Don't trust a single number from any blog, including this one.

On top of capital, budget for: SSM incorporation (typically RM2,500–RM4,000 including company-secretary fees and government charges), your commercial premises (rent and setup), the TTMC course (a roughly two-day course, commonly around RM800 per person), association membership, and the RM100 application fee. A realistic all-in figure to actually open and operate — capital, deposit, setup and a working-capital buffer — is often cited in the region of RM80,000–RM180,000 for a lean Malaysian-owned start, most of which is the refundable/usable capital rather than sunk cost.

What documents you'll need

One of the most common reasons applications get delayed is incomplete documentation. Prepare these before you apply:

  • Certified true copies of your SSM company documents (registration certificate and company details)
  • Colour copies of the passports or ICs of all directors and shareholders
  • A business plan covering your background, proposed activities, target markets and projected operations
  • Draft tour package proposals (inbound and/or outbound, matching your licence scope)
  • Proof of premises — a tenancy agreement (usually a minimum one-year lease) or proof of ownership, plus an office floor plan and interior/exterior photos
  • Your TTMC certificate and proof of tourism-association membership
  • Any letters of collaboration with travel partners, where relevant

A licensed company secretary will assemble most of this with you; getting it right the first time is the single biggest thing you can do to avoid weeks of back-and-forth.

How long does it take?

Most operators should plan for two to four months from starting company registration to holding a licence. It helps to separate two things here: MOTAC's own client charter for approving a new tour operating licence is 35 days from the receipt of a complete application — but that clock only starts once everything is submitted correctly. The full end-to-end time is longer because of what comes before: company registration (around one to two weeks), assembling documents, and the premises inspection, which is zoned by state and can add anywhere from about two weeks to two months on its own. Having every document prepared correctly before you apply is the most reliable way to keep it on the shorter end.

Once licensed, note the renewal cycle: MOTAC licences are renewed on a one-to-three-year period depending on the renewal conditions, and renewals use the TTEC course rather than the TTMC.

After you're licensed: getting ready to take bookings

Getting the licence is the hard part. Once it's done, the next step is actually taking bookings — and this is where a lot of newly licensed operators lose easy money. Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between MOTAC and TOBTAB?
MOTAC is the Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture — the government body that regulates and licenses tour and travel businesses in Malaysia. TOBTAB (Tour Operating Business and Travel Agency Business) is the name of the actual licence MOTAC issues. So you apply to MOTAC for a TOBTAB licence; people often just call it "the MOTAC licence."
How much does a MOTAC licence cost?
The application fee is RM100, but the real cost is the paid-up capital you must show — for a Malaysian-owned company, commonly from around RM50,000 up to RM150,000–RM200,000 depending on your operating area (City Council areas tend to be higher than District Council areas) and licence scope. There's also a separate security deposit of around RM20,000, plus setup costs like SSM incorporation (RM2,500–RM4,000) and the TTMC course (around RM800 per person). Foreign-owned companies face much higher capital, from roughly RM500,000 up to RM1,500,000 by ASEAN status and ownership share. These figures vary by area and change over time — confirm the current requirement for your situation with MOTAC or a licensed company secretary.
How much paid-up capital do I need for a MOTAC licence?
It depends on where you operate and your licence scope, which is why MOTAC doesn't publish one flat figure. For Malaysian-owned companies it commonly ranges from around RM50,000 to RM150,000–RM200,000, with premises in City Council areas generally requiring more than District Council areas. The capital sits in your company bank account as usable company funds; MOTAC checks your bank statement to confirm it's there. Foreign-owned companies need substantially more.
How long does it take to get a MOTAC licence?
Plan for around two to four months from company registration to licence. The premises inspection — scheduled by state zone — is usually the biggest variable and can add several weeks on its own.
Do I need a physical office for a MOTAC licence?
Yes. MOTAC requires a physical commercial premises in a commercial (not residential) area, with a permanent address, and it must be inspected before your licence is granted.
Do I need to be a Sdn Bhd to get a MOTAC licence?
Yes. You must have a registered private limited company (Sdn Bhd) incorporated with SSM, with the business activity specifically stating tour operating and/or travel agency business. Sole proprietorships and partnerships don't qualify.
What is the TTMC course?
The TTMC (Travel and Tours Management Course) is a mandatory, MOTAC-accredited course that at least one key person in your company must complete and pass to get a new tour operating licence. For licence renewals, the equivalent is the TTEC (Travel and Tours Enhancement Course).
Can a foreigner own a Malaysian tour company?
Foreign ownership is possible, but it comes with much higher capital requirements and additional conditions, and the rules depend on ASEAN status and ownership share. For certain scopes and government-linked programmes, Bumiputera shareholding may be advantageous or required. This is a situation to work through with a licensed company secretary or corporate advisor rather than relying on general guidance.
