How Much Does a Website Cost in Malaysia? (2026 Price Guide)

Blake Ng
Co-founder, Kong Last updated: June 2026 15 min read

"How much does a website cost in Malaysia?" has no single answer, because it depends entirely on how you build it — and the range is enormous, from free to over RM100,000. This guide breaks down what each route actually costs in 2026, the hidden ongoing fees nobody mentions upfront, and an honest answer to the question most guides avoid: whether you need to pay anyone at all.

The short version: a freelancer runs roughly RM900–8,000 a project; an agency RM2,200–10,000 for a standard small-business site (much more for custom); a DIY builder RM100–300 a month; an AI builder around RM10–140 a month; and some tools are free. The right number depends on whether you need a custom-built brand asset or just a professional site that works — and for a lot of small and booking businesses, the honest answer is you don't need to pay an agency at all.

Option 1: Hire a web design agency — RM2,200 to RM100,000+

An agency is what most businesses assume they need, and for some they're the right call. A standard small-business website (5–10 pages, template-based, with responsive design, basic SEO and a CMS like WordPress) typically runs RM2,200–10,000 in Malaysia, according to 2026 pricing guides from Malaysian agencies including Aleph Media and Specflux. Custom-designed sites with bespoke layouts and lead-generation features run RM8,000–35,000+, and large or enterprise builds can exceed RM50,000–100,000 (Specflux, BikeBear, WDD, 2026).

What you're paying for at the higher end isn't just the build — it's strategy, custom design, copywriting, a team of specialists, project management, and accountability. That's genuine value if your website is a core sales-and-marketing asset for your business. The honest question is whether yours is. A café, a tour operator, or a tradesperson rarely needs a RM15,000 custom build to do what their site actually has to do.

Option 2: Hire a freelancer — RM900 to RM8,000

A freelance web designer is the middle path: cheaper than an agency, more hands-on than DIY. Malaysian freelancers typically charge RM80–300 per hour, or roughly RM900–8,000 per project depending on scope — generally 30–50% less than an agency for a similar site, per 2026 freelancer-vs-agency cost analyses from Specflux and XBTECH Studio. Freelancers suit straightforward projects with a clear scope and limited ongoing changes.

The trade-offs to know: a freelancer quote usually excludes hosting and maintenance (so budget those separately), and you're relying on one person for support, which can be a single point of failure if they get busy or move on. Great for a clean, simple build; less ideal if you need ongoing accountability.

Option 3: Build it yourself with a website builder — RM100 to RM300/month

Modern website builders (Wix, Squarespace, Shopify, or local options like SiteGiant) let you build a professional site yourself with no coding, on a monthly subscription of roughly RM100–300. There's no upfront design fee — you pick a template and customise it. For a small business that's comfortable spending a weekend on it, this is dramatically cheaper than hiring anyone, and the results are perfectly professional.

The catch is your time, and that global builders are priced in US dollars and don't support local Malaysian payment methods (FPX, Touch 'n Go, e-wallets) without an added gateway. We compare the main options in our honest comparison of the best website builders in Malaysia.

Option 4: Build it with AI — RM10 to RM140/month (or free)

The newest and often cheapest route is an AI website builder. Tools like Hostinger's AI builder, Wix's AI, and others now generate a complete site — layout, copy, even images — from a few prompts about your business, in minutes. Pricing runs from around RM10/month (Hostinger on a long commitment) up to RM100–140/month for the bigger names, and most have a free tier (with ads and a builder subdomain) — figures drawn from 2026 AI-builder pricing analyses by GigaPress, Kleap and ChilledSites. For speed and low cost on a simple site, it's genuinely impressive — you can have something live the same afternoon.

Be clear-eyed about the limits, though, because this is where AI builders are oversold. They're template-based generators: excellent for a simple brochure site, a portfolio, or a landing page, but weaker on custom functionality, bespoke design, and anything beyond the standard layout. Most also bundle the same caveats as the global builders — USD pricing, no native Malaysian payment methods, and renewal rates that jump after the first term. An AI builder is a great way to get a good-looking simple site fast and cheap; it's not a substitute for an agency when you genuinely need custom work.

And one limitation matters most if you're a booking business: an AI builder generates a static marketing site, not a booking engine. It can write you a beautiful page about your tours — but it can't give you capacity per session, live dates and time-slots, deposits, no-oversell logic, or FPX checkout. For that you still need a booking-native tool. AI is brilliant for drafting your words and your look; it doesn't solve taking the booking.

Option 5: Free — yes, genuinely free for some businesses

Here's the part the agency guides won't tell you: some businesses can get online and take payments for free. The "free" website builders you'll see advertised (Wix's free tier and similar) usually come with ads, a builder-branded web address, and no ability to take payments — so for a real business they tend to become paid plans. But a newer model is genuinely free: tools that make their money on a small transaction fee instead of a subscription.

For a booking business — a tour operator, an activity provider, a class or workshop host, an equipment rental — Kong is free for the business: no setup fee, no monthly fee, with guests paying just a 1.8% fee at checkout on online bookings (walk-in, phone and WhatsApp bookings are free). You connect your own Stripe or Xendit account, so local payment methods like FPX, Touch 'n Go, ShopeePay and DuitNow QR work out of the box. It's a booking site rather than a custom-branded corporate brochure, but for a business that mostly needs to look good and take bookings, it does the whole job for nothing upfront.

A smart combination, in fact: use an AI tool to draft your copy and photos, and a booking-native tool to actually take the bookings — both free or near-free, no agency required.

Why the price range is so huge (and what actually drives your quote)

Ask three Malaysian web designers for a quote and you might get RM700, RM8,000 and RM40,000 for what sounds like the same project. That's not because two of them are overcharging — it's because "a website" can mean wildly different amounts of work. Five factors set your price:

  1. Page count. The most direct driver. Each page adds roughly RM200–800 in production cost on a typical agency quote (more for complex pages). A 5-page site is genuinely cheaper than a 15-page one — more layouts, more content, more testing.
  2. Template vs custom design. A templated build (WordPress + a theme) costs a fraction of a fully custom-coded design. Custom typically costs 3–5× a templated build for the same page count. For most SMEs, a custom-designed site on a template framework gives most of the value at a fraction of the cost.
  3. E-commerce or special functionality. A shopping cart, payment gateway, inventory, bookings, or membership logic adds real cost — commonly RM2,000–10,000+ on top, depending on complexity.
  4. Who provides the content. If you hand over finished copy and photos, the build is faster and cheaper. If the designer has to write and source everything, add RM200–800 per page for copywriting and photography.
  5. Ongoing support. A one-off build with no support is cheaper upfront; a relationship with maintenance, updates and optimisation costs more but keeps the site alive. (More on why that matters under total cost of ownership, below.)

Before you spend a ringgit, know what you're actually buying: in The Digital State of Malaysian Tour Operators 2026, among operators that do have a live website, 70% still cannot take a booking on it — the site takes enquiries only (Kong, 2026, n=66). A site you paid for that can't take a booking is the most expensive kind.

Notice the jump between a basic site and an SME corporate site — that gap is where most Malaysian businesses sit, and it's where quotes vary most, because "corporate site" can mean anything from a stretched template to a fully custom build.

Why a cheap quote is cheap (the rows they skip)

Here's the insight that saves Malaysian SMEs the most money. When one vendor quotes RM2,000 and another RM8,000 for the same 10-page scope, the cheaper one usually isn't more efficient — they're skipping line items. A full website build has roughly six cost components: design, development, content (copywriting), testing/QA, project management, and post-launch support. A RM2,000 quote for a RM8,000 scope typically drops content (you write it yourself), testing (your customers find the bugs), and project management (you chase the freelancer on WhatsApp). The work doesn't disappear — it moves onto your plate. So before comparing two quotes, ask both to itemise the fee into those components; missing rows explain most price gaps better than "overcharging" does.

This cuts both ways, and it's good news if you're a small or booking business: if you're happy to write your own content, do your own testing, and manage it yourself — which is entirely doable with a builder or a booking-native tool — then you're not getting a worse website by paying less. You're just doing the rows yourself, for free.

A note on AI and price

You'll hear that AI has made websites cheap. The honest reality for professional builds is more nuanced: AI has made designers faster, not necessarily cheaper. Tools like AI code assistants cut development time, but agencies tend to pour that saved time into better sites — tighter code, more testing, better mobile performance — rather than slashing prices. AI genuinely does lower the cost at the DIY end (the AI builders in Option 4), but at the agency end it mostly raises quality rather than dropping the quote. So "just use AI" is a real cost-saver if you're building it yourself, and much less of one if you're hiring a pro.

The costs nobody quotes you upfront

Whatever route you choose, the sticker price is rarely the full price. Budget for these ongoing costs, because they're where "cheap" websites get expensive (figures below per Malaysian 2026 cost guides from ZenWeb, GoDaddy and Aleph Media):

  • Domain name: RM60–120 per year (around RM60 for a .com, RM80 for a .com.my, RM120 for a .my).
  • Hosting: RM200–2,900 per year, depending on whether you're on shared or managed hosting. (DIY builders and booking-native tools bundle this in — there's nothing separate to buy.)
  • Maintenance: RM100–800 per month if you pay someone to keep an agency or WordPress site updated, secure and backed up.
  • Content: copywriting and photography can add RM200–800 per page if you don't provide your own — and good photos matter enormously, especially for tour and hospitality businesses.

This is why a "RM2,000 website" can quietly become a RM5,000–6,000 first year once hosting, maintenance and a domain are added — and why builders and booking-native tools, which bundle hosting and maintenance into one price (or into nothing), often work out cheaper over three years than they first appear.

The number that actually matters: 3-year total cost

The sticker price is only year one. The honest way to compare your options is total cost of ownership over three years — build (or first-year cost) plus the recurring domain, hosting, maintenance and update costs. Looked at this way, the cheap option and the expensive option often swap places:

Two honest takeaways. First, a cheap one-off build isn't as cheap as it looks once three years of hosting, maintenance and an eventual redesign are added. Second — and this is the part the agency guides skip — the genuinely lowest three-year cost belongs to the DIY, AI and free booking-native routes, because they bundle the running costs in (or remove them entirely). For a booking business, Kong's three-year cost is just the 1.8% on the bookings you actually take; there's no fixed cost to total up at all.

Do you actually need to pay anyone?

This is the question worth sitting with before you spend a ringgit. Agencies and freelancers earn their fee on sites that are genuine, custom, lead-generating business assets — a corporate site with bespoke design, complex functionality, integrations, and ongoing optimisation. If that's what your business needs, pay for it; it's worth it.

But a lot of Malaysian small businesses — and especially booking businesses like tours, activities, classes and rentals — don't need that. They need a site that looks professional, explains what they offer, and lets customers book or pay. Modern DIY and AI builders do the first part for RM10–300 a month (or free), and for a booking business a tool like Kong does the whole job free. You'd be paying an agency thousands for something you could now generate with AI in an afternoon, or set up yourself for free.

A simple test: if you could describe everything your website needs to do in a few sentences, and none of it involves custom-built functionality or a brand that needs bespoke design work, you almost certainly don't need to hire anyone. Build it yourself, or use a free booking-native tool, and put the thousands you'd have spent into your actual business.

It ranges from free to over RM100,000 depending on how you build it. More than the build fee. Add domain (RM60–120/yr), hosting (RM200–2,900/yr), maintenance (RM100–800/mo for agency or WordPress sites) and an eventual redesign. Is it cheaper to use a website builder or hire a designer?
A website builder is almost always cheaper. A DIY builder costs RM100–300 a month with no upfront design fee, while hiring a designer or agency runs from hundreds to tens of thousands of ringgit upfront plus ongoing maintenance. The trade-off is your time — you build it yourself — but for a straightforward business site the result is just as professional.
What are the hidden costs of a website in Malaysia?
The common ones are domain registration (RM60–120/year), hosting (RM200–2,900/year), maintenance (RM100–800/month for agency or WordPress sites), and content like copywriting and photography (RM200–800/page). These can double the real first-year cost of a "cheap" website. DIY builders and booking-native tools bundle hosting and maintenance in, so there's less to add.

Yes. AI website builders (Hostinger's AI, Wix AI and others) generate a complete simple site from a few prompts in minutes, costing roughly RM10–140 a month, with free tiers that carry ads and a builder subdomain. A smart, near-free combination is to use AI for your copy and design and a free booking tool like Kong to take the bookings.

For many small businesses, you don't need to pay. DIY builders are inexpensive, and for booking businesses (tours, activities, classes, rentals) a booking-native tool like Kong is free to set up and use, with guests paying a small 1.8% fee on online bookings only. You'd mainly pay an agency if you need a custom-designed, complex, lead-generation site.

A small business typically spends between RM680 and RM3,020 a year if using affordable routes (a DIY builder plus domain), per GoDaddy's 2026 Malaysia cost guide — or free if it's a booking business using a tool like Kong. Hiring an agency for a custom small-business site pushes that to RM2,200–10,000 upfront plus ongoing costs. Most small businesses don't need the agency route to get a professional, working site.
