How to Start a Tour Business in Malaysia (2026)

Blake Ng
Co-founder, Kong June 2026 14 min read

Last updated: June 2026

With Visit Malaysia 2026 driving a wave of tourism, there's never been a better time to turn your idea — ATV trails, a cooking class, a guided jungle trek, island hopping, a heritage walk — into a real tour business. This guide takes you through the whole journey, from idea to your first paid booking, with the specific, non-obvious moves that save new operators months of wasted effort and lost bookings.

Here's the honest overview: you'll pressure-test your niche, lock down your name properly, register a company, get licensed by MOTAC, sort premises and insurance, get found online, set up bookings, and start your review flywheel. Let's go through each — with the details most "how to start" guides skip.

Step 1: Pick a niche you can win — and pressure-test it before you commit

Start with one experience you can run brilliantly, not ten you can run adequately. The operators who do well in Malaysia own a specific thing — ATV trails in Selangor, mangrove kayaking in Langkawi, a terrarium workshop in KL, jeep tours in the Cameron Highlands. Your niche is what makes a traveller choose you over the dozen generic operators in the same spot.

Before you spend a ringgit, pressure-test the idea like an investor would:

  • Check the demand is real. Search your activity plus your destination on Google ("kayaking Langkawi," "things to do in Cameron Highlands") and look at what already ranks on Google Maps — how many operators, how many reviews, how recent. Then check the OTAs: see what's actually selling on Klook, Viator, GetYourGuide and Tripadvisor for your area, what they charge, and read their 3-star reviews — those tell you exactly where existing operators fall short and where your gap is.
  • Find the underserved angle, not just the activity. "Island hopping in Langkawi" is saturated; "small-group island hopping for families with young kids, with floats and a slower pace" is a niche you can own. The reviews of your competitors will hand you these gaps for free.
  • Know your real capacity per session. How many guests can you genuinely take at once and still deliver a 5-star experience? Be honest — this single number drives your pricing, your staffing, and how you set up bookings later.
  • Price for profit, not just to undercut. Work out your cost per guest (fuel, guide, equipment, the 1.8% booking fee, insurance allocation) and price with a real margin on top. Check what the OTAs list similar experiences for and price near that for direct bookings — undercutting the market on your own channel just trains guests to expect cheap and leaves money on the table. Remember the OTAs take 20–30% commission; selling direct at the same price the guest sees on Klook means that margin stays with you.

Step 2: Lock down your name — check the company, the domain, and the handles together

Most operators pick a name, register the company, and only later discover the .com is taken or the Instagram handle belongs to someone else — then they're stuck rebuilding their brand after they've already earned reviews and rankings under the old name. Do it in the right order, all at once, before you commit to anything:

  1. Check the company name is available on SSM's MyCoID portal (you can reserve it for around RM50 for 30 days). MOTAC expects the name to clearly relate to tours or travel, so words like "Tours," "Travel," "Adventures" or "Holidays" help.
  2. Check the domain in the same sitting — ideally the .com, and the .com.my. If `yourname.com` is gone, that's often a reason to tweak the name now rather than later. A clean, matching domain makes you look established and makes word-of-mouth easy ("just go to ___.com").
  3. Check the social handles — Instagram, TikTok and Facebook — for the same name. For a tour business, Instagram and TikTok are where your photos sell trips, so a consistent handle across all three matters more than it does for most businesses.
  4. Only then register the Sdn Bhd. You'll need a registered private limited company (a Sdn Bhd, incorporated with SSM) to get licensed — a sole proprietorship won't qualify for the tour operating licence. At registration, your SSM business activity needs the specific wording about carrying on tour operating and/or travel agency business; we cover the exact phrasing in the full MOTAC licence guide.

Getting all four aligned before you incorporate costs you an afternoon. Getting it wrong costs you a rebrand after you've built an audience.

Step 3: Get licensed with MOTAC

If you're running tours and taking payment, you need a MOTAC licence (the TOBTAB licence) to operate legally. This is the step that takes the most time and money, so start it early.

In brief, getting licensed means: your Sdn Bhd, a physical commercial premises that MOTAC inspects, completing the mandatory TTMC course, joining a recognised tourism association, and applying through MOTAC's TOURLIST portal. The full walkthrough — including capital requirements and timelines — is in our MOTAC licence guide, and if the acronyms are confusing, our TOBTAB licence explainer untangles them.

Step 4: Sort your premises

MOTAC requires a physical commercial premises — in a commercial area, not residential, with a permanent address — and they'll inspect it before licensing you. Plan for this early, because the inspection is scheduled by state zone and can add weeks to your timeline. (Details in the MOTAC guide.)

Step 5: Get insured

Don't skip this. If you're taking guests on ATVs, boats, kayaks or jungle treks, things can go wrong, and the right cover protects both your guests and your business. At a minimum, look into public liability insurance, and ask your insurer about cover specific to your activity (water sports, off-road and adventure activities often need their own policies). Talk to an insurer or broker who understands the tourism and adventure space — it's a small cost next to the risk of running uninsured, and some partners and venues will require proof of cover before they work with you.

Step 6: Get found online with a Google Business Profile

For most tour operators, your Google Business Profile is how international guests will find you — it's free, and it's the highest-leverage thing you can set up early. When someone searches "ATV Selangor" or "things to do in Langkawi," a well-set-up profile is what puts you in the map results. It's no small thing: many of Kong's operators get around 60% of their direct bookings from their Google Business Profile.

The move most new operators miss: pick the handful of keywords your future guests actually type ("mangrove kayaking Langkawi," "Cameron Highlands jeep tour") and work those exact phrases into your profile — your business description, your services, and especially your Activities section (which unlocks when you set your category to "Tour operator"). Setting the category alone isn't enough; the keywords have to appear in the content. Add real photos of your actual experience, not stock. We walk through the keyword research and exactly where to put the terms in our Google Business Profile and local SEO guide for tour operators — set this up before you spend a ringgit on advertising, because it's free traffic you're otherwise leaving on the table.

Step 7: Get a way to take bookings — you can be live in minutes

This is the step new operators most often get wrong, and it's one of the most important. "DM us on WhatsApp to book" quietly loses you international guests — travellers from the UK, US, Japan, Australia and Germany often don't use WhatsApp and won't download it to book a tour. It also loses you bookings at 2am while you're asleep, and the ones who never message because they wanted to just book and pay there and then. You need a real booking page where anyone, anywhere, can book and pay in the moment they decide.

Step 8: Get your first bookings and start the review flywheel

Here's the opportunity in one number: per Kong's operator research, 92% of Malaysian tour operators cannot take an online booking anywhere they own — no website, a dead site, or an enquiry-only site (Kong, 2026, n=252). Take online bookings from day one and you're ahead of almost the entire market.

With a Google Business Profile and a booking link, you have everything you need to start. Your first bookings won't come from advertising — they come from a few specific moves:

  • Seed your first reviews legitimately. You can't buy reviews or fake them (Google will catch it), but you can run your first few trips for friends, family and locals at cost, deliver brilliantly, and ask each of them to leave an honest Google review. Five or six genuine reviews is the difference between a profile travellers trust and one they scroll past — it's the hardest part of the cold start, so engineer it deliberately.
  • Get listed where travellers already look. Beyond your own channels, list on the OTAs (Klook, Viator, GetYourGuide) for reach and on Tripadvisor for discovery. Yes they take commission, but early on they're a visibility engine — and every guest they send you is someone you can convert into a direct, review-leaving, repeat customer.
  • Ask for the review at the right moment. Ask in person, right at the end of the experience, while the guest is still buzzing — a QR code that opens your review page converts far better than an email three days later. This is the single highest-return habit you can build.
  • Reply to every review, good and bad. It signals to Google you're active and shows future guests how you treat people.

Reviews lift your Maps ranking and build the trust that converts the next browser into a booking — so each happy guest makes the next one easier to win. That's the flywheel; the sooner you start it, the sooner it carries you. (Our guide to getting more Google reviews goes deeper on the mechanics.)

A quick-start checklist

  1. Product — one experience you can run brilliantly, with a clear price and capacity.
  2. Company — register your Sdn Bhd with SSM.
  3. Licence — get your MOTAC (TOBTAB) licence; start early.
  4. Premises — commercial premises, ready for MOTAC inspection.
  5. Insurance — public liability and activity-specific cover.
  6. Get found — set up your Google Business Profile.
  7. Take bookings — a free Kong booking page, live in minutes.
  8. First bookings — Google, your booking link, reviews, word of mouth.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a tour business in Malaysia?
The biggest cost is getting licensed. The MOTAC application fee is RM100, but you'll need to show paid-up capital — for a Malaysian-owned company, commonly from around RM50,000 up to RM150,000–RM200,000 depending on your operating area and licence scope — plus a separate security deposit of around RM20,000. On top of that, budget for company registration (RM2,500–RM4,000), premises, the TTMC course (around RM800 per person), insurance, and association membership. Taking bookings, by contrast, can be free — Kong's booking software has no operator fees. Confirm current licensing figures with MOTAC, as they vary by area.
How do I choose a name for my tour business?
Pick a name that clearly signals tours or travel (MOTAC expects this), then — before you register anything — check three things together: that the company name is free on SSM's MyCoID portal, that the matching .com (and .com.my) domain is available, and that the Instagram, TikTok and Facebook handles are free. Aligning all of these before you incorporate saves you from an expensive rebrand after you've built reviews and a following under a name you can't fully own online.
How should I price my tours?
Start from your real cost per guest (fuel, guide, equipment, insurance, the booking fee) and add a genuine margin. Check what comparable experiences sell for on Klook, Viator and GetYourGuide and price near that on your direct channel — don't undercut your own bookings, because the OTAs charge guests a similar price while taking 20–30% commission, so selling direct at the market price keeps that margin with you. Pricing too cheap signals low quality and leaves money on the table.
Do I need a licence to run tours in Malaysia?
Yes. If you operate tours and take payment, you need a MOTAC licence (the TOBTAB licence) to operate legally. See our full MOTAC licence guide for the process.
Can I run tours as a sole proprietor, or do I need a Sdn Bhd?
For a MOTAC tour operating licence, you need a registered private limited company (Sdn Bhd). A sole proprietorship doesn't qualify for the licence.
Do I need a website to take bookings?
No. You don't need to build a full website to start taking bookings — a booking page is enough, and Kong gives you one for free that you can set up in minutes. You can add website pages (About, galleries) later if you want them, since Kong is also a simple website builder.
How do I get my first bookings?
Seed genuine early reviews by running your first trips for friends, family and locals and asking for honest Google reviews; get listed on OTAs like Klook and Viator for reach; and put your booking link everywhere (Google profile, Instagram/TikTok bio, WhatsApp status). Then ask every happy guest for a review in person at the end of the trip — that review flywheel is what makes each subsequent booking easier to win.
When is the best time to start a tour business in Malaysia?
2026 is a strong year to start, with Visit Malaysia 2026 driving tourism demand and government promotion of the sector. Getting licensed and online before and during the campaign puts you in front of the increased visitor numbers.
