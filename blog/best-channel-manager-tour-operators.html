The Best Channel Managers for Tour Operators in 2026 (And Whether You Need One at All)

Blake Ng
Co-founder, Kong Published July 6, 2026 Figures checked 3 July 2026 ~24 minute read

Disclosure first, opinions second

I spent six years inside the tour booking software industry, at Rezdy, Checkfront and Regiondo. I sold the platforms, marketed them, and watched which features actually helped operators and which ones were just demo theatre. Today I co-run a booking platform called Kong. We are building a channel manager of our own, but it is not publicly available yet, so it is not in this comparison and nothing here is graded against it. When it launches, I will add it to this page and say so plainly.

I'm telling you this in the first paragraph because the rest of this article only works if you trust that I'm being calibrated and honest. Every platform on this list has real strengths and real weaknesses. I'll name both, and where the honest answer is "it depends on what kind of operator you are," I'll say that too. If you're looking for a generic top eight listicle, this isn't it.

How we put this together

Everything in this guide comes from each company's public pricing pages, documentation, channel directories, and public user reviews, checked on 3 July 2026. Review patterns come from Capterra, which carries the deepest review bases for these products, cross checked against G2 where G2 has enough volume. One caution on both: a large share of reviews on these platforms are vendor referred and incentivized, which the platforms disclose per review. We lean on non incentivized reviews and the platforms' own aggregate summaries, and treat no single review site as gospel. Vendors change pricing and ship features without announcements. If you are evaluating seriously, confirm the specifics with the vendor before you sign. And if you spot something that has changed, tell us and we will update the page.

Key takeaways

Each line below stands on its own. All figures checked 3 July 2026.

  • A channel manager keeps a tour operator's availability in sync across every channel they sell on, so a booking anywhere closes the seat everywhere.
  • The short answer to which is best: Rezdy for the largest reseller network, Bókun if Viator is your main OTA, GlobalTix for Asian OTA reach including Traveloka, Ventrata for large attractions, and none of them if you sell on fewer than three channels.
  • Based on publicly available information, today's tour channel managers sell connectivity, not control. None of the eight platforms reviewed publishes hotel style control features such as seat or date allotments per channel. You are on a channel or you are not.
  • Content sync is the category's other missing layer. Availability syncs everywhere, prices sync on some connections, and descriptions and photos almost never sync, which is why a separate product category (Magpie) exists to fill the gap.
  • Most operators selling on one or two OTAs do not need a channel manager yet. The tool starts paying for itself at around three or more active channels with real volume on each.
  • Published pricing runs from $49 to $569 per month, and nearly every platform adds per booking fees on top, from 1% to 3% or a flat $0.50 to $1.50 per booking. GlobalTix and BeMyGuest do not publish pricing.
  • Of the eight platforms reviewed, only GlobalTix publishes a Traveloka connection, and none publishes a KKday connection. The category's coverage goes quiet exactly where Southeast Asian demand lives.

What a channel manager does

A channel manager keeps your availability in sync across every place you sell. Your website, GetYourGuide, Viator, Klook, a hotel concierge desk. When a seat gets booked anywhere, it closes everywhere.

Without one, you update each channel by hand, and the gap between updates is where double bookings live.

That is the definition every vendor gives, and it is accurate. It is also only half the job. This section stays brief on purpose: if you want the full explanation of how the software works, read our guide to what a channel manager is, then come back for the comparison.

A true channel manager has both connectivity and control

A true channel manager should do two things.

Connectivity is selling through multiple channels. This is what every product on this page markets. Connect once, appear on many shelves.

Control is deciding what each channel gets. Which dates each channel may sell. How many seats each channel may sell per departure. What price each channel shows. When to stop a channel selling without pulling your whole listing down.

Hotels have had this for decades. A hotel channel manager lets a revenue manager give Booking.com thirty rooms for March and Agoda ten, then shut one channel off for a peak weekend. Allotments and stop sells are standard hotel technology.

Tour operators got the shallow copy. In most tour channel managers today, a channel is a switch. You are on GetYourGuide or you are not. Rate control exists in places. Rezdy lets you negotiate rates per agent. Bókun lets you set prices and commissions per sales channel. But seat level and date level allotment control, the thing hoteliers take for granted, is not offered publicly by any of the eight platforms we reviewed. On the public record, the category today sells connectivity. Control barely exists. If a vendor tells you otherwise, ask them to show it to you on a live departure.

The three layers of sync

Even within connectivity, "synced" means three different things, and most tools only mean the first.

Layer 1: availability. A booking on any channel closes the seat on every channel. This is the baseline. All eight platforms below do this for their connected channels.

Layer 2: price. Your rates flow from one system to every channel. Some connections do this. Rezdy's Klook connection pulls live pricing. Bókun's live integrations sync directly, but its marketplace connections work on contracted retail rates you maintain in the agreement. On many platforms, a price change still means logging into each OTA extranet.

Layer 3: content. Descriptions, photos, meeting points, FAQs. Almost no channel manager pushes these through. The proof is that an entire company exists to fill this exact gap. Magpie is a content management platform for tours whose pitch is "save once, update everywhere," pushing text and images to Viator, GetYourGuide, Expedia and Musement. Its founder describes operators with twenty or more channel partners, all needing up to date information, all maintained by hand. Magpie was seed funded by the founder of Viator. The people who built OTA distribution know content sync is the missing layer, because they built a separate business to patch it.

So when a vendor says "everything syncs," ask which layer they mean. Usually it is availability. Sometimes it is price. It is almost never content.

Not every network is the same

One more thing the marketing glosses over: what a channel manager connects you to varies wildly. Some are OTA pipes and nothing more. Others carry a full trade network behind the OTAs: travel agents, DMCs, hotel concierge desks, tourism boards, transport providers, sometimes airlines.

That difference decides which tool fits you. If your growth plan is OTA led, judge the OTA list and the sync depth. If your growth plan is trade led, meaning inbound agents, hotels reselling your tours to their guests, or destination partners, then the reseller network is the product and the OTA list is a bonus. Rezdy's marketplace claims over 20,000 agents and resellers, from hotel concierges to tourism bodies. Bókun's marketplace claims 2,600 plus resellers including travel agents, DMCs, accommodation providers and transport companies. GlobalTix claims over 8,000 travel agents across Asia. Those are three different shapes of network, and only one of them might match how your bookings actually arrive.

Do you actually need one?

Here is the answer nobody selling a channel manager will give you: often, not yet.

Work through four questions.

How many OTA listings do you actually run? If you are on one or two OTAs, you can manage availability by hand or with each OTA's own calendar tools. The admin is annoying but small. A channel manager starts paying for itself around three or more active channels with real volume on each.

How many bookings per channel per month? Ten bookings a month across two channels is a spreadsheet problem, not a software problem. A hundred bookings across four channels is a channel manager problem.

Is your availability pooled or per departure? If every channel sells from one shared pool of seats, sync matters enormously and a channel manager helps. If you run private tours priced per group, double booking risk is lower and the tool matters less.

How often do you actually get double booked? If the answer is never, you do not have the problem this software solves. Do not buy insurance against a fire you have never had.

And one question that comes before all four: can guests book you directly at all? If a guest who finds you cannot book you, distribution software is solving the wrong problem first. Fix the direct channel, then distribute.

Rezdy: the category's biggest name

Best for

Operators who want the largest reseller marketplace.

Rezdy is the loudest name in tour channel management. It is an Australian platform that merged with Checkfront and Regiondo in 2023, and channel management is its flagship. Two things separate it from the rest of this list. First, the marketplace: over 20,000 agents and resellers, and not just OTAs. Hotel concierges, tourism bodies, local information centres, inbound tour operators. If your growth plan runs through the trade rather than through consumer OTA listings, this network is the product. Second, you do not need to run your whole business on Rezdy to use it. The channel manager works standalone, either through a separate login alongside your existing booking system or through the RezdyConnect API for a full integration.

The OTA side is solid. The Klook connection is documented in Rezdy's own support pages and pulls live pricing and availability with instant bookings, which is deeper sync than most connections on this page manage. Rate control is real too. You set marketplace rates with your own commission, and you can negotiate specific rates per agent.

Pricing model

Pricing is $49, $99 or $249 per month. Online bookings carry a 3% fee at the entry tier. Agent bookings carry a flat $0.85 fee each, and offline bookings carry a per booking fee that steps down by plan from $1.00 to $0.70.

Checked 3 July 2026 against the vendor's public pages.

What 100 bookings a month costs

On the $49 plan, 3% on $1,600 of direct online sales is $48, and 60 agent bookings at $0.85 is $51. Roughly $148 a month, before OTA commissions.

What reviewers say

Rezdy holds 4.6 stars on Capterra, and the channel manager is the most consistently praised piece. Operators describe stable API connections and real time OTA inventory, and one large Chicago cruise operator credits it with growing their OTA connections by 300%. The recurring complaints run the other way on usability and cost: the platform is complex to learn, "intimidating at first" is a common phrase and one reviewer compared parts of the interface to Windows 95, small operators say essential features sit behind higher plans, and support stories swing between "responsive and proactive" and slow email queues. The sharpest recurring lesson is about the marketplace: being connectable to 20,000 agents does not mean agents will sell you. One operator with a five star tour reported contacting hundreds of agents with almost no uptake. Reach is an invitation, not a pipeline. G2's aggregate reads the same way as Capterra's, praising ease of use and flexibility while flagging the learning curve, and G2's head to head comparison finds reviewers preferring Bókun over Rezdy on ongoing support quality and product direction.

ProsThe biggest and broadest reseller network here. Documented Klook connection with live pricing. Per agent negotiated rates. Usable alongside another booking system.
ConsThe fee stack. Rezdy's per booking fee lands on top of the OTA's own commission, so an OTA booking pays twice. Modelling your real cost takes a spreadsheet, which is rarely a good sign in a pricing page.

Bókun: best if you live on Viator

Best for

Operators whose main OTA is Viator.

Bókun is owned by Tripadvisor, which also owns Viator, and the whole product makes most sense read through that fact. Viator bookings carry 0% Bókun fees. There is a free plan that manages Viator and offline bookings only. Bókun even advertises a premium connectivity arrangement with GetYourGuide. If Viator is your volume, no other platform on this page comes close on cost.

Know what kind of connection you are buying, though. Bókun's live integrations, the direct availability sync, cover exactly three channels: Viator, GetYourGuide and Expedia Local Expert. Everything else, including Klook and Trip.com, connects through the Bókun marketplace as a contracted reseller. Those connections run on contract terms and retail rates you maintain in the agreement, at the standard booking fee. It works, but it is a different, shallower thing than the live sync the marketing leads with.

The rest of the product is more than a channel pipe. It is a booking system with resource management built in: guides, vehicles and equipment tracked against bookings, mass closeouts for maintenance, and per channel price and commission settings. The reseller marketplace claims over 2,600 partners including travel agents, DMCs, accommodation providers and transport companies. Third party reviews put full setup at one to three weeks depending on product complexity.

Pricing model

Pricing is $49, $149 or $499 per month plus a per booking fee of 1.5%, 1.25% or 1% by tier. Those are the lowest booking fees in the category.

Checked 3 July 2026 against the vendor's public pages.

What 100 bookings a month costs

On the $49 plan, 1.5% on $4,000 is $60, so about $109 a month. Now make the 60 OTA bookings Viator bookings: the fee only touches the 40 direct ones, $24, so about $73 a month. That gap is the Tripadvisor relationship, priced.

What reviewers say

Support is Bókun's strongest reviewed feature. Capterra's own aggregate summary finds most reviewers call support reliable, quick to respond and helpful, and individual reviews repeat it constantly. The same summary also finds most reviewers report bugs and technical issues occurring frequently with slow resolution. That is an unusual combination: friendly firefighters, recurring fires. Setup takes learning. Reviewers describe the first weeks as confusing, and several report OTA connections that showed as connected but were not properly set up, costing them bookings before anyone noticed. Two review complaints matter beyond Bókun: operators asking for per departure pricing and reseller allotments, which are not available, and operators discovering a product cannot be created once and exported to other OTAs, so pricing gets recreated platform by platform. The control and content gaps this article describes are not our theory. Operators are writing them into the reviews. On G2, the picture holds: in G2's own Bókun versus Rezdy comparison, reviewers preferred Bókun on quality of ongoing support and on feature direction.

ProsCheapest per booking fees here, and 0% on Viator. Real per channel price and commission control. A genuinely broad reseller network beyond OTAs. Free plan to start.
ConsYour distribution tool and your biggest sales channel answer to the same parent company. That is a dependency to think hard about before you build on it. And Klook is a marketplace connection, not a live integration.

TourCMS: the distribution veteran

Best for

Operators with a defined, stable set of channels, especially into European resellers.

TourCMS, owned by Swiss firm Palisis, has been doing tour distribution longer than almost anyone on this list. The client roster tells you who it is for: it is the channel manager behind Gray Line licensees worldwide, it has run a Civitatis partnership for over five years, and destination organisations like Turisme de Barcelona use it to distribute third party products. This is trade grade plumbing with a long memory.

Named connections include GetYourGuide, Viator, Expedia and Hotelbeds, that last one being a bedbank the others here mostly ignore. Palisis also markets dynamic pricing on top of the channel manager, their claim, worth testing in a demo.

Pricing model

The pricing model is the most predictable on this page because plans are capped by channel count: Small at $49 a month covers 2 channels and 30 bookings, then $159, $329 and $569 tiers raise the caps to 5, 20 and 25 channels. On top sits a 1.9% distribution fee on bookings from connected partners. Yearly billing gets two months free.

Checked 3 July 2026 against the vendor's public pages.

What 100 bookings a month costs

The $49 plan caps at 30 bookings, so you need Standard at $159. Add 1.9% on $2,400 of partner bookings, about $46. Roughly $205 a month.

ProsPredictable channel capped pricing. Deep distribution pedigree, Gray Line and Civitatis grade. Hotelbeds reach the others lack.
ConsThe caps bite exactly when you want to experiment with a new OTA. The entry tier's 30 booking cap rules out most working operators. Klook, Traveloka and KKday connections are not published.

Ventrata: built for a different customer

Best for

Large attractions and hop on hop off operators. Not for independent SEA operators.

Ventrata is enterprise software and does not pretend otherwise. Public listings put plans from roughly €525 per month plus a 2% transaction fee. G2 lists editions running from $550 to $5,500 a month, contracts are quoted, and implementation fees are typical. The product is built around high volume ticketing: hop on hop off routes, big attractions, city cards. It connects to the major Western OTAs by API, and reviewers rate it highly for what it is.

What 100 bookings a month costs

From about €525 plus 2% on €4,000, so roughly €605 a month. At this volume you are paying enterprise rent for a kampung stall. That is not a criticism of Ventrata. It is a criticism of putting it on your shortlist.

What reviewers say

Ventrata holds 4.8 stars on Capterra across a small base of 18 reviews, which is consistent with an enterprise product bought through sales processes rather than self serve signups. A thin public review base is normal at this end of the market; ask for reference customers instead.

ProsGenuine enterprise capability. Strong for high volume, multi gate ticketing.
ConsPriced and built for a customer most independent operators are not. Real pricing needs a sales conversation.

TrekkSoft: read the fee schedule twice

Best for

European operators already inside its ecosystem.

TrekkSoft is Swiss, Europe focused, and the fee structure is the story. There are three layers. First the subscription: €49, €149 or €249 per month, billed annually, with a 12 month contract required. Then channel fees on every booking: €0.50 to €1.50 on offline bookings, and 2% to 3% on online and OTA bookings depending on tier. Then payments: TrekkSoft's own Payyo gateway at 2.5% plus €0.25 per transaction inside Europe, more outside, and Stripe or PayPal are only available on the top plan. TrekkSoft also raised prices sharply on legacy customers in 2023, which is documented and worth knowing before signing a 12 month commitment.

Connections include GetYourGuide, Viator and Expedia through its own help pages. The European reseller network is established and real.

What 100 bookings a month costs

On the €149 plan, channel fees at 2.5% on €4,000 are €100. Payyo on the €1,600 of direct sales adds about €50. Roughly €299 a month, and that is before noticing you are locked in for a year.

What reviewers say

TrekkSoft has around 230 reviews on Capterra, and they split into two populations. Operators with a dedicated account manager write glowing reviews, often naming their rep. Operators in the general support queue describe a different company: days for a ticket to reach support, replies taking a week or more, and one long term customer reporting the service getting worse year by year. The fee structure draws its own complaints, including fees on offline bookings, charges on refunds, and the Payyo currency conversion default, which one operator documented double converting a US dollar card paying for a US dollar tour. The checkout is also criticised for allowing only one product per booking. The pattern to take seriously: your experience appears to depend heavily on which support tier your contract buys.

ProsAn established European reseller network. Booking system included.
ConsThree separate fee layers on one booking. A 12 month contract before you know if it fits. Payment processing locked to Payyo except on the top plan. SEA OTA connections are not published.

Regiondo: solid, and pointed at Europe

Best for

Operators in the DACH region, France, Italy or Spain.

Regiondo is a European booking and distribution system, part of the Rezdy group since the 2023 merger. Its own positioning names Germany, Austria, Switzerland, France, Italy and Spain as home turf, and the reseller relationships follow the positioning. Connections include GetYourGuide, Viator, Expedia and Tripadvisor.

Pricing model

The pricing stacks: €49, €99 or €199 per month, plus a €0.49 fee per ticket, plus a 3% system usage fee. A website builder is a further €49 a month if you want it. Payment processing runs through Regiondo's Stripe based setup only. Stripe supports Malaysia, so it technically works here, but a Stripe only, Europe pointed product is answering a different market's question.

Checked 3 July 2026 against the vendor's public pages.

What 100 bookings a month costs

On the €99 plan, ticket fees add about €49 and the 3% usage fee on €4,000 adds €120. Roughly €268 a month.

What reviewers say

Regiondo holds 4.2 stars across 54 Capterra reviews. The praise is consistent: easy to embed in a website, quick and friendly support, and solid Viator and GetYourGuide integrations. The complaints cluster on reliability and edges. Several reviewers report the booking widget breaking so customers could not book at all. One UK operator describes a failed migration with pricing miscalculations and package deals stuck showing fully booked. OTA API connections get described as trial and error, with some OTAs connecting and some not. Small details also give away the home market, such as report exports in German number formatting that UK spreadsheets cannot read. The happiest reviewers are in Regiondo's home DACH market. The unhappiest are outside it, which matches the positioning point above.

ProsCapable system with a real European reseller network. Backed by the Rezdy group.
ConsThree stacked fees. Built and marketed for a different continent. SEA OTA connections are not published.

GlobalTix: the Asia native

Best for

Southeast Asian operators who want Asian OTA reach.

GlobalTix is the entrant most relevant to Malaysian operators, and the only one headquartered in this region's reality. Singapore HQ, ten offices across Asia including Malaysia, and scale that is easy to underestimate: over 150,000 experiences, more than 8,000 travel agents in its network, and millions of tickets a year. Its published channel list names Expedia, Trip.com and Traveloka. That last name is the one to notice. Traveloka carries an enormous share of Southeast Asian bookings, and no Western platform on this page publishes a connection to it.

The commercial model is different too. GlobalTix runs direct contracts: you negotiate your own terms with each OTA and distributor, and GlobalTix provides the pipes and the single dashboard. Your OTA relationships stay yours. For operators who have been burned by platforms sitting in the middle of their commercial relationships, that structure is the point.

Pricing model

Pricing is not published. It is quote based, so ask directly and put the quote next to the worked examples above with your own volumes.

Checked 3 July 2026 against the vendor's public pages.

What reviewers say

We did not find a public review base deep enough for a fair pattern summary. For a platform this established in the Asian trade, that says more about who its customers are (businesses signed through sales teams, not self serve) than about the product. Ask for operator references in Malaysia; GlobalTix has an office here.

ProsGenuinely Asian distribution, with Traveloka named and offices in Malaysia. Direct contract model keeps your OTA relationships yours. Scale that rivals the Western names.
ConsNo public pricing, so comparison requires a sales conversation. GetYourGuide, Viator and Klook connections are not published, so if your demand is Western OTAs, verify the pipes before assuming them.

BeMyGuest: the Asia distribution veteran

Best for

Attractions and operators selling heavily into the Asian B2B trade.

BeMyGuest is the other Singapore name, and one of the earliest companies in Asian experiences distribution. Its Xplore reservation system launched in 2018, and its B2B network is trusted enough that major attraction brands use BeMyGuest as their regional channel manager. This is attraction grade infrastructure with a decade of Asian trade relationships behind it.

Its leadership has also been publicly vocal about independence: the argument that operators should think twice about distribution tools owned by the OTAs they distribute through. In a category where the second biggest product is owned by Tripadvisor, that is not an abstract point.

Pricing model

Pricing is not published, and the public materials do not itemise the channel network, so treat both as questions for the sales call.

Checked 3 July 2026 against the vendor's public pages.

What reviewers say

We did not find a public review base deep enough for a fair pattern summary. Same read as GlobalTix: trade software bought through relationships, not review sites. Ask for reference customers.

ProsLong Asia track record and attraction grade distribution. Independent ownership, and unshy about why that matters.
ConsAttraction leaning rather than small operator leaning. No public pricing, no published channel list.

The ninth option: manage your OTAs directly

Nobody selling software will list this option, so we will. If you are on one or two OTAs, you can run them from their own extranets. Klook, GetYourGuide and Viator all provide supplier dashboards with calendars, pricing tools and blocked dates. It costs nothing. The admin grows with each channel you add, and somewhere around channel three the maths starts favouring software. Until then, direct management is not a failure state. It is the right size of tool. What this means for Malaysian and SEA operators

The channel manager category was built around Western OTAs for Western operators. Klook dominates Asia Pacific among the big OTAs, Traveloka carries huge Southeast Asian volume, and the connectivity table above shows how thinly the category covers them. If you are still choosing which OTAs to sell on, our comparison of GetYourGuide, Viator and Klook covers that decision.

Meanwhile, the Asian OTAs are not waiting. Klook and KKday are building their own supplier tools. Rezdy's own leadership has spoken about why Western reservation technology has struggled in Asia, pointing at localisation, payment complexity and commercial viability. The honest summary for a Malaysian operator in 2026 is this: if your volume is Western tourists booking on GetYourGuide and Viator, the Western platforms serve you fine. If your volume is regional travellers on Klook and Traveloka, your realistic options are GlobalTix, direct OTA management, or waiting for the category to catch up to your market.

And if you cannot yet take a booking on a channel you own, that is the first fix, before any of this. Distribution multiplies what you have. If direct guests cannot book you, there is nothing to multiply.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best channel manager for tour operators?

It depends on where your bookings come from, and anyone giving one answer is selling something. Rezdy has the largest reseller network. Bókun is the cheapest by far if Viator is your main OTA. GlobalTix is the only platform reviewed that publishes a Traveloka connection, which matters for Southeast Asian demand. Ventrata suits large attractions. And if you sell on fewer than three channels, the best channel manager is usually none: manage your OTA listings directly for free.

Do small tour operators need a channel manager?

Usually not at first. If you sell on one or two OTAs with modest volume, their own supplier dashboards are enough. A channel manager starts earning its cost at around three or more active channels, or when double bookings start actually happening.

How much does a channel manager cost for tour operators?

Published subscriptions run from about $49 to $569 per month, and nearly every platform adds per booking fees on top, ranging from 1% to 3% or a flat $0.50 to $1.50 per booking. The Asian platforms, GlobalTix and BeMyGuest, do not publish pricing. Model your real monthly volume before comparing, because the per booking fees usually matter more than the subscription.

Does Bókun connect to Klook?

Yes, through the Bókun marketplace as a contracted reseller. It is not one of Bókun's live integrations. Those are Viator, GetYourGuide and Expedia Local Expert.

Does Rezdy connect to Klook?

Yes. Rezdy publishes a Klook connection guide, and the connection pulls live pricing and availability with instant bookings.

Which channel managers connect to Traveloka?

Of the eight platforms we reviewed, only GlobalTix publishes a Traveloka connection. No Western platform on this list does.

What is the difference between a channel manager and an OTA?

An OTA is a shop that sells your tours to travellers and takes a commission. A channel manager is the plumbing that keeps your availability and rates in sync across several of those shops at once. You can be on OTAs without a channel manager. You cannot use a channel manager without channels.

Can a channel manager update my prices and photos on OTAs automatically?

Prices, sometimes. It depends on the specific connection, so ask about the channel you care about, not about syncing in general. Photos and descriptions, almost never. Content distribution is a separate category, which is why tools like Magpie exist.

Which channel manager has the best support?

On public reviews, Bókun's support is the best reviewed in the category: Capterra's aggregate summary finds most reviewers call it reliable, quick and helpful. Rezdy's support reviews are mixed, running from "responsive and proactive" to slow email queues. For the quote based platforms, ask what support is contractually included, because at enterprise pricing it is negotiable.

What is the difference between an OTA connection and a reseller network?

An OTA connection puts your tours on a consumer marketplace like Klook or Viator. A reseller network connects you to the trade: travel agents, DMCs, hotels and tourism boards who sell your tours to their own customers. Some channel managers only do the first. If inbound agents or hotel partnerships drive your bookings, the network matters more than the OTA list.

What is the difference between a hotel channel manager and a tour channel manager?

Hotel channel managers have mature control features: room allotments per channel, stop sells, rate plans per channel. Tour channel managers mostly offer connectivity, meaning you are on a channel or you are not, with rate control on some platforms and no published seat level allotment control on any platform we reviewed.
