Tour and activity glossary

Plain definitions of 137 words tour and activity operators run into every day, from pax, FOC and net rates to TOBTAB licences, e-invoicing and ATDW listings. Each entry gives a one-line definition and a real tour example, with extra detail for operators in Malaysia and Australia.

0-9

3D Secure Payments

3D Secure is an extra check during an online card payment, such as a code sent to the guest's phone or a prompt in their banking app.

Also called: 3DS

It cuts card fraud and, in many cases, moves the cost of fraud chargebacks away from the business.

See also: Chargeback, PCI DSS

A

Abandoned cart Payments

An abandoned cart is a booking a guest starts online but doesn't finish.

Also called: abandoned booking

Common causes are a surprise fee, a missing payment method, or a slow booking page on a phone. Some booking systems send a reminder email.

See also: Booking form, Booking fee, Payment gateway

Add-ons Pricing

Add-ons are optional extras a guest can buy with a booking, such as hotel pickup, equipment hire, meals or photos.

Also called: extras · In Bahasa Malaysia: tambahan

They're offered during checkout or after booking, and each one is priced separately from the tour.

See also: Upselling, Transfer

Affiliate booking Selling and distribution

An affiliate booking is a booking sent to an operator by a partner, such as a blogger, hotel or website, who earns a commission for it.

Also called: affiliate · In Bahasa Malaysia: tempahan affiliate

The partner shares a tracked link or books on the guest's behalf. The operator needs to know which affiliate each booking came from, so the right commission gets paid at the end of the month.

See also: Commission, Reseller, Concierge booking

Allocation Bookings and the day

An allocation is a set number of places kept aside for a particular partner or channel, such as an agent or OTA.

Also called: allotment, block · In Bahasa Malaysia: peruntukan

It gives the partner confidence it can always sell, but places it doesn't sell can go empty unless there's a release date when they return to general sale. Many operators now prefer shared live availability to fixed allocations.

See also: Inventory, Availability, Reseller

ATDW Rules and licences

ATDW (the Australian Tourism Data Warehouse) is Australia's national database of tourism listings, which shares operators' details with state tourism websites, Tourism Australia and other partners.

Also called: Australian Tourism Data Warehouse, ATDW listing

An operator creates one listing, with descriptions, photos, prices and booking links, and ATDW sends it out to many websites, including each state's official tourism site and Australia.com. For a small operator, it's one of the simplest ways to appear on the sites visitors and trade buyers actually use when planning. Listings are managed through ATDW's online portal, and state tourism bodies check new listings. Keeping the listing current matters: an old price or a dead booking link looks bad on an official site. Some state tourism bodies also run campaigns open only to operators with an ATDW listing. Operators outside Australia can't list, but Australian partners of Malaysian operators, such as inbound agents, may ask whether a product is on ATDW before including it in their programmes.

See also: Tourism Australia, QTIC

Source: ATDW, checked September 2026

Atome Payments

Atome is a buy now pay later provider in Southeast Asia that lets shoppers split a payment into instalments.

The business is paid upfront, and Atome collects the instalments from the shopper.

See also: Buy now pay later

Availability Bookings and the day

Availability is the number of places still open to book for a particular date and time.

Also called: live availability, real-time availability · In Bahasa Malaysia: ketersediaan

It changes every time someone books or cancels, on any channel. Showing live availability everywhere, on the operator's own website and on each OTA, is what prevents overbooking. Guests read it as a signal too: a session with two places left gets booked faster.

See also: Capacity, Inventory, Overbooking

B

Balance payment Payments

The balance payment is the rest of the price, paid after the deposit, usually by a set date before the tour.

Also called: balance, final payment · In Bahasa Malaysia: baki bayaran

Operators send a reminder or a payment link before it's due, and state in the terms what happens if it isn't paid.

See also: Deposit, Payment link

Blackout dates Pricing

Blackout dates are days when a discount, voucher or deal can't be used, or when a tour isn't available at all.

In Bahasa Malaysia: tarikh blackout

Operators usually black out public holidays and peak dates from promotions, and close dates for maintenance, weather or private charters. The dates should be clear before the guest pays, so they don't find out at checkout.

See also: Peak season, Gift voucher, Promo code

Booking engine Selling and distribution

A booking engine is the software that lets guests pick a date, see what's available, and book and pay online.

Also called: online booking system, booking system · In Bahasa Malaysia: enjin tempahan

It's the part of a booking system guests see. Behind it, the operator's side handles the calendar, payments and guest details. A good booking engine works well on phones, shows live availability and takes payment in the method the guest prefers.

See also: Booking widget, Availability, Payment gateway

Booking fee Pricing

A booking fee is a charge added when a guest books online, paid on top of the tour price.

Also called: processing fee, convenience fee · In Bahasa Malaysia: yuran tempahan

Some booking systems charge it to the guest instead of the operator. It must be shown before payment.

See also: Surcharge, Merchant fee

Booking form Bookings and the day

A booking form is the set of questions a guest answers when booking, from names and contact details to anything the operator needs to know.

Also called: custom fields, booking questions · In Bahasa Malaysia: borang tempahan

Good forms ask only what's needed: dietary needs, the hotel for pickup, weight or height for safety limits, or swimming ability. Long forms lose bookings, especially on phones. Questions the operator only needs later, like T-shirt sizes for a team event, can be collected after booking by email instead. Anything personal, such as health details or ID numbers, should be asked for only when it's genuinely needed.

See also: Abandoned cart, Child policy, PDPA

Booking link Selling and distribution

A booking link is a web address that takes a guest straight to the booking page for a tour or activity.

Also called: QR booking · In Bahasa Malaysia: pautan tempahan

Operators share it on Instagram, WhatsApp and their Google Business Profile, or print it as a QR code at the counter or on flyers.

See also: Direct booking, Booking engine

Booking reference Bookings and the day

A booking reference is the unique code given to each booking, used to find it quickly.

Also called: booking number, confirmation number · In Bahasa Malaysia: nombor rujukan tempahan

Guests quote it when changing a booking, and operators use it at check-in and in any dispute.

See also: Check-in, Manifest

Booking vs reservation Bookings and the day

A booking and a reservation mean the same thing for most tours: a guest's place held for a date and time.

In Bahasa Malaysia: tempahan dan reservasi

Some businesses use reservation for an unpaid hold and booking once it's paid, so it's worth saying which you mean.

See also: Deposit, Instant confirmation

Booking widget Selling and distribution

A booking widget is a small piece of code that puts a booking engine inside an operator's own website.

In Bahasa Malaysia: widget tempahan

Guests choose a date and pay without leaving the operator's site, which keeps the brand in front of them and usually converts better than sending them elsewhere.

See also: Booking engine, Direct booking

Booking window Bookings and the day

The booking window is how far ahead guests can book a tour, from the furthest date open for sale to the cut-off time.

Also called: lead time · In Bahasa Malaysia: tempoh tempahan

It's also used to mean how far ahead guests typically book, which helps with pricing and staffing.

See also: Cut-off time, Early bird discount

Buy now pay later Payments

Buy now pay later (BNPL) lets a guest split a payment into instalments, while the business is paid upfront by the BNPL provider.

Also called: BNPL, instalment payment · In Bahasa Malaysia: beli sekarang bayar kemudian

It can help guests afford higher-priced trips and group bookings. The provider takes on the risk of the guest not paying and charges the business a fee. In Malaysia, providers such as Atome are offered through some payment gateways.

See also: Atome, Payment gateway

C

Cancellation policy Bookings and the day

A cancellation policy sets out what happens if a guest cancels or doesn't show up, and what happens if the operator cancels.

Also called: cancellation terms · In Bahasa Malaysia: polisi pembatalan

A typical policy has steps: a full refund if the guest cancels well ahead, a partial refund closer to the date, and nothing for late cancellations or no-shows. Group and charter bookings often have stricter terms, because the operator turns other guests away for them. The policy should also cover what happens when the operator cancels, for weather or too few guests, which usually means a full refund or a free change of date. OTAs often apply their own rules to bookings made through them, so operators line their policies up to avoid confusion. The policy needs to be short, plain and shown before payment, not buried in the terms. Clear policies cut arguments and chargebacks, because the guest agreed to them when booking.

See also: Refund policy, Force majeure, No-show

Capacity Bookings and the day

Capacity is the most guests a tour or session can take.

In Bahasa Malaysia: kapasiti

It's usually set by the tightest limit: seats in the vehicle, places on the boat, life jackets, or guests per guide. Some tours have more than one limit at once, and the booking system should stop sales as soon as any of them is reached.

See also: Availability, Resources, Overbooking

Chargeback Payments

A chargeback is when a guest asks their bank to reverse a card payment, and the bank takes the money back from the business.

Also called: payment dispute · In Bahasa Malaysia: caj balik

Guests usually do it when they say a tour didn't happen, was cancelled without a refund, or wasn't what was promised. The operator can dispute it with evidence: the booking record, the terms the guest accepted, check-in records and messages. Chargebacks usually carry a fee, even when the operator wins.

See also: Refund policy, 3D Secure, Terms and conditions

Charter Bookings and the day

A charter is the private hire of a boat, vehicle, guide or whole tour by one group.

Also called: private charter, private hire · In Bahasa Malaysia: sewa khas

The group pays for the whole thing rather than per seat, and usually chooses the time and sometimes the route. Charters are common for boats, fishing trips and island hopping. Operators usually take a deposit and set clear terms for weather cancellations.

See also: Deposit, Force majeure, Rain check

Check-in Bookings and the day

Check-in is when guests arrive and are marked as present before a tour starts.

In Bahasa Malaysia: daftar masuk

It's also when operators confirm names, signed waivers and payment.

See also: Manifest, Headcount, Liability waiver

Child policy Bookings and the day

A child policy sets the rules and prices for children on a tour: minimum age, age bands, prices, and whether an adult must come along.

In Bahasa Malaysia: polisi kanak-kanak

Operators often set a child price, free places for infants, and safety limits like minimum height or swimming ability. Stating how age is counted, such as age on the day of the tour, avoids arguments at check-in.

See also: Infant, Concession, Pax

Commission Selling and distribution

Commission is the share of the price an OTA, agent or partner keeps for selling a booking, usually a percentage of the public price.

Also called: travel agent commission · In Bahasa Malaysia: komisen

OTAs and resellers work on commission, and so do hotel concierges, affiliates and many travel agents. There are two common set-ups. In the first, the partner collects the full price from the guest and passes the operator the rest after taking its commission. In the second, the operator collects the full price and pays the partner's commission later, often monthly. Commission is the main reason direct bookings earn operators more. When setting prices, operators check that a booking still leaves enough margin after commission to cover guides, fuel, insurance and payment fees, which is where net rates and commissionable rates come in. Many Malaysian operators still settle commission with small partners by hand, so recording who sent each booking matters.

See also: Commissionable rate, Net rate, OTA (online travel agent)

Commissionable rate Pricing

A commissionable rate is a public price that includes the seller's commission, which is paid out of it after the sale.

In Bahasa Malaysia: harga berkomisen

The guest pays the same price they'd see on the operator's website. The agent or OTA keeps its commission, and the operator receives the rest. This is the usual model for OTAs and many travel agents, and it keeps prices the same everywhere. The difference from a net rate is where the seller's income comes from. With a commissionable rate, it's inside the public price. With a net rate, the reseller adds its markup on top of the operator's price. Before agreeing to a commission, operators check that what's left still covers the cost of running the tour, including guides, fuel and payment fees. Hotels use the same term for rooms.

See also: Commission, Net rate, Rack rate

Concession Pricing

A concession is a reduced price for certain guests, such as children, students, seniors or pensioners.

Also called: student discount, senior discount, concession price · In Bahasa Malaysia: harga konsesi

The word is common in Australia. In Malaysia, operators more often list child, senior or student prices. Operators usually ask for proof, like a student card, at check-in. Whatever the name, each reduced price needs clear rules on who qualifies and how age is counted, shown before the guest pays.

See also: Child policy, Pax

Concierge booking Selling and distribution

A concierge booking is one made by a hotel concierge or front desk on behalf of a hotel guest.

In Bahasa Malaysia: tempahan concierge

Hotels are a steady source of bookings for day tours and activities, especially from guests who decide on the spot. The hotel usually earns a commission, and the booking needs the guest's name, hotel and pickup details.

See also: Affiliate booking, Commission, Fam trip

Contracted rate Pricing

A contracted rate is a price agreed in writing with a specific partner, usually for a set period.

In Bahasa Malaysia: harga kontrak

DMCs and travel agents often fix contracted rates a year ahead, so they can price their packages and brochures with confidence.

See also: Net rate, DMC (destination management company)

Credit note Payments

A credit note is a document that reduces or cancels an amount already invoiced, for example after a cancellation or a price change.

In Bahasa Malaysia: nota kredit

Instead of editing the original invoice, the operator issues a credit note against it. Depending on the terms, it records a refund or gives the guest credit for a future booking. Accountants and tax systems expect it, including Malaysia's e-invoice system.

See also: Refund policy, Invoice vs receipt, E-invoice (LHDN)

Cut-off time Bookings and the day

The cut-off time is the latest point before a tour when bookings close.

In Bahasa Malaysia: masa tutup tempahan

Operators set it so they have time to prepare, such as ordering food, arranging pickups or confirming a boat.

See also: Booking window, Last-minute booking

D

Day trip, half-day and full-day Bookings and the day

A day trip starts and ends on the same day; half-day tours usually take a few hours, and full-day tours most of the day.

Also called: day tour, half-day tour, full-day tour · In Bahasa Malaysia: lawatan sehari

The terms are used loosely, so listings should state the actual duration and start and finish times.

See also: Itinerary, Transfer

Deposit Payments

A deposit is part of the price paid when booking, to secure the place, with the balance paid later.

Also called: down payment · In Bahasa Malaysia: bayaran pendahuluan

Deposits protect operators from no-shows and late cancellations, especially for private charters, groups and multi-day trips where costs are paid in advance. The terms need to be clear before payment: how much the deposit is, when the balance is due, and whether the deposit is refunded if the guest cancels. For corporate and MICE groups, the deposit is often requested with a proforma invoice, and the booking is confirmed once it's paid. For short day tours, most operators take full payment at booking instead, which is simpler and cuts no-shows further. Whatever the set-up, the booking record should show clearly what has been paid and what is still owed, so staff on the day don't have to chase guests for money at check-in or rely on memory and WhatsApp threads.

See also: Balance payment, Cancellation policy, No-show

Direct booking Selling and distribution

A direct booking is one a guest makes with the operator itself, through its website, phone, WhatsApp or counter, with no OTA or agent in between.

In Bahasa Malaysia: tempahan terus

No commission is paid, the operator keeps the guest's contact details, and it can invite them back. Direct bookings usually earn the most per guest, which is why operators work to grow them with a good website, an up-to-date Google Business Profile and repeat guests.

See also: OTA (online travel agent), Booking engine, Commission

DMC (destination management company) Selling and distribution

A DMC (destination management company) is a local business that plans and runs trips, events and ground arrangements in its own destination for overseas agents and groups.

Also called: destination management company, ground handler, inbound agent · In Bahasa Malaysia: syarikat pengurusan destinasi

A travel agent in Europe selling a Borneo trip usually doesn't deal with every hotel, driver and guide. It works with a DMC in Malaysia, which puts the programme together, books the local suppliers and handles everything on the ground. DMCs are big buyers of tours and activities: they book day trips, transfers and experiences for their groups, often at net rates and often months ahead. For an activity operator, one good DMC relationship can mean regular group bookings. DMCs expect clear net rates, reliable confirmations and invoices, and a named contact for each group. Many DMCs also handle MICE groups for companies.

See also: Net rate, MICE, PIC (person in charge)

Double booking Bookings and the day

A double booking is when the same place, boat, guide or time is sold twice.

In Bahasa Malaysia: tempahan bertindih

It usually happens when bookings live in more than one place, like a notebook, a WhatsApp chat and a website.

See also: Overbooking, Resources

DuitNow Payments

DuitNow is Malaysia's national instant payment service, run by PayNet, that lets people pay using a phone number, ID number or DuitNow QR code.

Also called: DuitNow QR

Almost any Malaysian banking or eWallet app can scan a DuitNow QR code.

See also: FPX, eWallet

Dynamic pricing Pricing

Dynamic pricing means changing a tour's price based on demand, such as charging more on busy weekends and less on quiet weekdays.

Also called: demand-based pricing · In Bahasa Malaysia: harga dinamik

Airlines and hotels have done it for years. For tours, it usually means a few simple rules: a higher price in peak season or for the last few places, a lower one for early bookings or slow days. It helps fill quiet sessions and earn more from busy ones.

See also: Revenue management, Peak season, Early bird discount

E

E-invoice (LHDN) Rules and licences

An e-invoice is an invoice issued in a set digital format and validated by LHDN, Malaysia's Inland Revenue Board, through its MyInvois system.

Also called: e-invoicing, MyInvois, e invoice · In Bahasa Malaysia: e-invois

Malaysia has been phasing in e-invoicing by business size since August 2024, starting with the largest companies and moving down to smaller ones, with the smallest businesses exempt under the current rules. Tour operators, travel agencies and hotels are covered like any other business, and LHDN has published guidance for tourism cases. In practice, a business in scope issues an e-invoice for each sale, or a consolidated e-invoice covering many small sales to guests who don't ask for their own. Buyers such as companies and government bodies may ask for an e-invoice with their tax number on it. Because the dates and turnover thresholds have changed more than once, operators check LHDN's current timeline for a business of their size.

See also: Invoice vs receipt, Credit note, Proforma invoice

Source: Inland Revenue Board of Malaysia (LHDN), checked September 2026

Early bird discount Pricing

An early bird discount is a lower price for guests who book well ahead.

In Bahasa Malaysia: diskaun early bird

It brings in cash early and helps operators plan staff, boats and supplies for the season.

See also: Dynamic pricing, Booking window

EFTPOS Payments

EFTPOS is Australia's debit card payment network, and also the everyday word for a card machine at a counter.

Australian guests may say they'll "pay by EFTPOS" when they mean tapping a card on the day.

See also: Surcharge, Merchant fee

eWallet Payments

An eWallet is a phone app that stores money or card details, so people can pay with a tap or a QR scan.

Also called: e-wallet, digital wallet · In Bahasa Malaysia: e-dompet

In Malaysia, Touch 'n Go eWallet, GrabPay, ShopeePay and Boost are widely used, and many local guests prefer them to cards.

See also: Touch 'n Go eWallet, GrabPay, ShopeePay

F

Fam trip Selling and distribution

A fam trip (familiarisation trip) is a free or discounted trip that lets travel agents, hotel staff or media experience a tour so they can sell or write about it.

Also called: familiarisation trip, familiarization trip, famil · In Bahasa Malaysia: lawatan pengenalan

People sell what they've tried. An agent who has done your mangrove kayak tour describes it better and recommends it with more confidence. Fam trips are often organised by tourism boards, DMCs and associations, and operators take part by hosting a group for free or at cost. They work at a smaller scale too: inviting the concierge teams of nearby hotels for a free session is one of the simplest ways to win hotel referrals. To make a fam trip pay off, give guests the same experience paying guests get, follow up with booking details and net rates, and track which partners book afterwards.

See also: Concierge booking, DMC (destination management company), Net rate

FIT (free independent traveller) Selling and distribution

FIT (free independent traveller) means a guest travelling on their own plan rather than with a group tour.

Also called: independent traveller, FIT traveller · In Bahasa Malaysia: pelancong bebas

FIT guests book tours one at a time, often at short notice and often on their phone. The opposite is a group booked through an agent.

See also: Last-minute booking, Group booking

FOC (free of charge) Pricing

FOC (free of charge) means a place given without payment, most often the free place a group leader, guide or driver gets on a group booking.

Also called: free of charge · In Bahasa Malaysia: percuma

In Malaysia, FOC is everyday booking language. A DMC might ask for "1 FOC per 15 pax", meaning every 15 paying guests earn one free place, usually for the tour leader. Operators also give FOC places to young children, on fam trips, or as a goodwill gesture after a problem. An FOC place still costs the operator a seat, a life jacket and a share of the guide's time, so the rule should be agreed before a quote is accepted: how many paying guests earn an FOC place, and whether it covers meals, transport and entrance fees. Many operators cap FOC places per booking and exclude them from any commission.

See also: Pax, Group booking, DMC (destination management company)

Force majeure Bookings and the day

Force majeure means events outside anyone's control, such as storms, floods, pandemics or government orders, that stop a service going ahead.

A force majeure clause says what happens then, usually that neither side is at fault and the guest gets a refund, credit or new date. For outdoor operators in Malaysia, monsoon weather is the most common case.

See also: Cancellation policy, Rain check, Terms and conditions

FPX Payments

FPX is Malaysia's online banking payment method: the guest picks their bank at checkout and approves the payment in their banking app.

Also called: Financial Process Exchange, online banking

It's run by PayNet and is one of the most used ways to pay online in Malaysia. The money comes straight from the guest's bank account with no card needed, which suits guests who don't have or don't want to use a credit card.

See also: DuitNow, Payment gateway, eWallet

Free and easy Pricing

Free and easy is a package with transport and a hotel but no set tour programme, so guests plan their own days.

The term is common in Malaysia and Singapore. Free and easy guests are prime customers for day tours booked after they arrive.

See also: Tour package, FIT (free independent traveller)

G

Gift voucher Pricing

A gift voucher is a prepaid credit or a specific experience that someone buys for another person to use later.

Also called: gift card, gift certificate · In Bahasa Malaysia: baucar hadiah

Vouchers bring in money before the tour runs and bring in new guests, especially around festive seasons and birthdays. Operators set an expiry date and any blackout dates up front, so there are no surprises when the voucher is used.

See also: Promo code, Blackout dates

Google Things to Do Selling and distribution

Google Things to Do is the part of Google Search and Maps that lists tours, activities and attractions, with prices and links to book.

When someone searches for an activity in a place, Google can show booking options from the operator's own website and from OTAs side by side. Operators appear through a connected booking system. A direct listing gives guests a way to book with the operator rather than through an OTA.

See also: Reserve with Google, Direct booking, OTA (online travel agent)

GrabPay Payments

GrabPay is the eWallet inside the Grab app, used across Malaysia and Southeast Asia.

Visitors from nearby countries often already have it on their phones.

See also: eWallet, Touch 'n Go eWallet

Gratuity Payments

A gratuity is a tip: money a guest chooses to give guides or crew on top of the price.

Also called: tipping · In Bahasa Malaysia: tip

Tipping habits differ by country. Malaysian guests don't always tip, while many visitors from North America expect to. If a gratuity is already included in the price, say so, so guests don't tip twice. Operators who pool tips should explain to staff how they're shared, and some booking systems let guests add a tip at checkout or after the tour.

See also: Service charge

Group booking Bookings and the day

A group booking is one booking for several guests travelling together, often arranged by one person, a company or an agent.

In Bahasa Malaysia: tempahan kumpulan

Groups often want a quote, a group rate, a private session or a set time, and pay a deposit and then the balance. One contact handles changes, and final numbers are usually confirmed a few days before the tour.

See also: PIC (person in charge), Quotation, Group rate

Group rate Pricing

A group rate is a lower price per person for groups above a set size.

In Bahasa Malaysia: harga kumpulan

Operators often pair it with a minimum group size, a deposit and a date by which final numbers must be confirmed.

See also: Group booking, FOC (free of charge), Deposit

H

Headcount Bookings and the day

Headcount is the number of people actually present, counted on the day.

In Bahasa Malaysia: bilangan peserta

It's checked against the manifest before departure and again at the end, especially on boats and treks.

See also: Manifest, Check-in, Pax

Homestay Rules and licences

In Malaysia, a homestay usually means a stay with a local family in a village, under MOTAC's registered homestay programme.

Guests live with the host family, share meals and join village activities, often as a group. Many private rentals also call themselves homestays but aren't part of the programme. Homestay villages often work with tour operators on packages for schools, companies and overseas groups.

See also: Tour package, Tourism tax

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture (MOTAC), checked September 2026

I

iCal Tech and AI

iCal is a standard file format for calendar events, used to share or sync bookings between calendars and systems.

Also called: iCalendar, .ics, calendar sync

Operators use iCal links to show bookings in Google Calendar, Apple Calendar or Outlook, or to block dates from another system. iCal syncs are usually one-way and can lag behind, so they suit viewing bookings more than keeping live availability.

See also: Availability, Double booking

Inbound tour operator Selling and distribution

An inbound tour operator sells tours in its own country to visitors arriving from abroad, often through overseas agents.

Also called: receptive operator · In Bahasa Malaysia: pengendali pelancongan masuk

Outbound operators do the reverse, sending local travellers overseas. In Malaysia, inbound is also one of the TOBTAB licence categories.

See also: DMC (destination management company), TOBTAB, FIT (free independent traveller)

Inclusions and exclusions Bookings and the day

Inclusions and exclusions list what the price covers and what it doesn't, such as transport, meals, entrance fees and gear.

Also called: what's included · In Bahasa Malaysia: termasuk dan tidak termasuk

Clear lists cut questions before booking and complaints after it.

See also: Itinerary, Add-ons

Indemnity form Bookings and the day

An indemnity form is a document in which the signer agrees to cover the operator's costs if their actions, or those of someone they're responsible for, lead to a claim.

Also called: indemnity letter · In Bahasa Malaysia: borang indemniti

In Malaysia, borang indemniti is the everyday word for what many operators use as a waiver, and schools often ask parents to sign one for trips. Strictly, an indemnity and a waiver do different jobs, and many forms include both.

See also: Liability waiver, Release of liability

Infant Bookings and the day

An infant is a very young child, usually under two, who often travels free or at a reduced price.

In Bahasa Malaysia: bayi

Operators set their own age limit and still count infants in the headcount for safety.

See also: Child policy, Headcount

Instant confirmation Bookings and the day

Instant confirmation means a booking is confirmed the moment the guest pays, with no wait for the operator to approve it.

In Bahasa Malaysia: pengesahan segera

Guests and OTAs increasingly expect it. It only works when availability is live on every channel.

See also: Availability, Last-minute booking

Inventory Bookings and the day

Inventory is everything an operator has to sell: every place on every session across the season.

In Bahasa Malaysia: inventori

The word comes from hotels and airlines. For tours, inventory is shared between channels: a place sold on an OTA is one less on the operator's website. Keeping one pool of inventory for every channel, rather than splitting it up, avoids both overbooking and empty seats.

See also: Allocation, Availability, Capacity

Invoice vs receipt Payments

An invoice asks for payment; a receipt confirms payment has been made.

In Bahasa Malaysia: invois dan resit

Operators send an invoice when payment is due later, as with company groups, and a receipt once the guest has paid, as with most online bookings. Companies often need both for their records. In Malaysia, e-invoicing rules may decide what form these documents take.

See also: Proforma invoice, E-invoice (LHDN), Credit note

Itinerary Bookings and the day

An itinerary is the plan of a trip: where guests go, in what order, and at what times.

In Bahasa Malaysia: itinerari

For a day tour, it might be a short timeline. For a multi-day trip, it's a day-by-day plan with meals and accommodation. Markup vs margin Pricing

Markup is how much you add to your cost to set a price; margin is how much of the final price is profit.

In Bahasa Malaysia: markup dan margin keuntungan

They sound alike but give different numbers. If a tour costs 60 to run and sells for 100, the markup is 67 percent (40 added on top of 60), but the margin is 40 percent (40 of the 100 is profit). Mixing them up costs money: an operator aiming for a 40 percent margin who adds a 40 percent markup ends up with a margin of about 29 percent. It matters most with resellers and OTAs, because their commission comes out of the price, so the operator needs enough margin to give some away and still cover guides, fuel, insurance and payment fees. Knowing the cost per pax and the margin at each price makes it easy to decide which partners and discounts are worth it.

Net rate Pricing

Also called: net price, wholesale rate · In Bahasa Malaysia: harga bersih

Tourism accreditation is certification that a tourism business meets set standards for things like safety, customer service and business practice.

Also called: accreditation, Quality Tourism Framework · In Bahasa Malaysia: akreditasi pelancongan

In Australia, the national Quality Tourism Framework includes accreditation programmes, and some parks, tourism bodies and grant schemes ask for it. It can also help with insurers and trade partners. In Malaysia, MOTAC licensing and industry certifications play a similar role.

See also: Public liability insurance, ATDW

Source: Australian Tourism Export Council (ATEC), checked September 2026

Tourism Australia Rules and licences

Tourism Australia is the Australian Government agency that promotes Australia to international visitors.

It runs Australia.com and trade events that connect operators with overseas buyers.

See also: ATDW, QTIC

Source: Tourism Australia, checked September 2026

Tourism Malaysia Rules and licences

Tourism Malaysia (the Malaysia Tourism Promotion Board) is the government agency that promotes Malaysia as a destination, at home and abroad.

Also called: Malaysia Tourism Promotion Board

It sits under MOTAC and runs national campaigns such as Visit Malaysia Year.

See also: MOTAC licence

Source: Tourism Malaysia, checked September 2026

Tourism tax Rules and licences

Tourism tax is a flat charge per room per night that foreign guests pay when staying at registered accommodation in Malaysia.

Also called: TTx, tourist tax · In Bahasa Malaysia: cukai pelancongan

It's collected by the accommodation, not the tour operator, and paid to the Royal Malaysian Customs Department. If you only run tours and activities and don't provide rooms, you generally don't collect it. Operators selling packages that include accommodation should check who collects it.

Read more: Tourism tax in Malaysia: what tour operators need to know

See also: SST, Tour package, Homestay

Source: Royal Malaysian Customs Department (MyTTx), checked September 2026

Tourist guide licence Rules and licences

A tourist guide licence is the licence MOTAC issues to individuals who guide tourists in Malaysia.

Also called: tour guide licence, tourist guide license · In Bahasa Malaysia: lesen pemandu pelancong

Guides usually complete a MOTAC-approved course first, and there are different types, such as city guides and nature guides, some limited to one area. Operators who employ guides check that each licence is valid, and many OTAs and DMCs ask about it.

See also: MOTAC licence, TOBTAB

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture (MOTAC), checked September 2026

Transfer Bookings and the day

A transfer is transport between two points on a trip, such as from a hotel or airport to the start of a tour.

Also called: hotel pickup, pickup and drop-off, airport transfer · In Bahasa Malaysia: pengangkutan

Operators state whether transfers are included, and from which areas.

See also: Add-ons, Meeting point

Travel agency Selling and distribution

A travel agency is a business that sells travel made by others, such as tours, flights and hotels, usually for a commission or markup.

Also called: travel agent · In Bahasa Malaysia: agensi pelancongan

Agents don't run the tour themselves. They book it for their customer and earn a cut, either as commission from the operator or by adding their own markup to a net rate. In Malaysia, travel agencies are licensed by MOTAC under the same TOBTAB system as tour operators, with separate categories for inbound, outbound and ticketing.

See also: Commission, Net rate, TOBTAB

Travel voucher Pricing

A travel voucher is a document or code a guest shows to prove they have paid for a service.

In Bahasa Malaysia: baucar pelancongan

Agents and DMCs often issue them to their guests for tours booked on their behalf, and the operator checks them at check-in.

See also: Check-in, DMC (destination management company)

Twin sharing Pricing

Twin sharing means a package price per person, based on two people sharing one room.

Also called: single supplement · In Bahasa Malaysia: bilik berkongsi

A guest travelling alone usually pays a single supplement to have the room to themselves.

See also: Tour package, Rooming list

U

Upselling Pricing

Upselling is offering a guest something extra, or a better version of what they're booking, such as a private boat or a photo package.

Also called: cross-selling · In Bahasa Malaysia: upsell

Offered at checkout, in the confirmation email or on the day, it lifts earnings per guest without finding new guests. It works best when the extra is genuinely useful, like transport, gear hire or a longer session.

See also: Add-ons

W

Waitlist Bookings and the day

A waitlist is a list of guests who want a tour that's full, in case places open up.

In Bahasa Malaysia: senarai menunggu

When someone cancels, the first guest on the list is offered the place.

See also: Availability, Capacity

Walk-in Bookings and the day

A walk-in is a guest who turns up and books on the spot, without booking ahead.

Recording walk-ins in the same system as online bookings keeps the headcount and remaining capacity right.

See also: Off-platform booking, Capacity

Yield management Pricing

Yield management is an older name for revenue management, first used by airlines to earn the most from each flight.

For tour operators, the two terms mean the same thing in practice.
