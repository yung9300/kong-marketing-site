The Digital State of Malaysian Tour Operators 2026 Edition 1

Only 8% of Malaysian tour operators can take an online booking.

We checked 252 tour, activity and experience operators across Malaysia, one by one. Only 8% can take a booking online on a channel they own. 74% have no website of their own at all. The rest use WhatsApp, Instagram, walk-ins, or someone else's platform.

The finding

Great ratings. Hundreds of reviews. No way to book online.

We looked at 252 independent Malaysian operators — dive shops, ATV parks, island boats, cooking schools, craft studios, jet-ski outfits. We checked each one by hand for one thing: can a guest complete a booking online, on a channel the operator owns? For only 8%, the answer is yes.

The other 92% have no website, a dead one, or a site that shows the packages but still sends every booking to WhatsApp. This is not a demand problem, and it is not a quality problem. The typical operator here holds a 4.7-star Google rating and hundreds of reviews. A Batu Ferringhi jet-ski operator with a 4.9 rating takes every booking over WhatsApp. A Bangsar perfume studio with 517 reviews works entirely through Instagram messages. A Balik Pulau horse stable with over 1,000 reviews has no website at all.

The guests want to book. There is no simple way to let them.

The booking gap

A website is usually not a booking.

74% of operators have no website of their own. They can be reached only through WhatsApp, Instagram, TikTok, Linktree, a Google Business Profile, or a marketplace. But having a website does not mean an operator can take a booking. The bigger surprise is what happens among the 26% who do have a live site.

We checked all 66 operators who have a working website, by hand. Only 20 of them can take a booking on it. The other 46 have a website that shows the packages, the photos and the price, then asks you to message to book. The site is a brochure. Across the full sample of 252, that is how the bookable share comes to 8%.

By activity type · the signature data

It is not one flat number.

The pattern is not the same across every activity. It splits by what the operator sells. Activities that need a licence, a certification, or a fixed destination — diving, cooking, rafting — are more likely to have a real site. Activities run by a single owner selling a spontaneous experience are almost all offline.

Horse riding, escape rooms, watersports and craft workshops run 80–100% offline. Diving, cooking schools and white-water rafting have the most websites, and even in those categories roughly half the operators have none. Shares for the smallest categories (fewer than 10 operators) are directional; the headline figures do not depend on them.

"No website of their own" counts operators reachable only via WhatsApp, Instagram, TikTok, Linktree, a Google Business Profile alone, or third-party marketplaces. Operators who sell through an OTA but have no site of their own are counted as offline.

Consistent across the country.

The pattern holds from the Klang Valley to Borneo. It is not a rural-versus-urban story — a Kuala Lumpur craft studio is as likely to be offline as a kampung boat operator.

Methodology

How we did it.

1
Census, not survey. We built the sample by searching Google Maps for tour, activity and experience operators across 14 Malaysian regions and 14 activity types — the way a traveller actually finds them.
2
Inclusion. Independent operators selling a bookable experience: dive centres, ATV parks, boats, workshops, cooking classes, horse stables, go-kart tracks and more. We excluded hotels, attractions, museums, national parks and umrah/ticketing agencies.
3
Website verification. For each operator we checked for a website of their own — an owned domain or site-builder subdomain — and confirmed indexation using Semrush's domain-rank data. A real indexed footprint counts as "has website"; social, messaging or marketplace presence alone counts as offline.
4
Booking-mechanism review. For all 66 operators with a working website, we then visited the site and checked, by hand, whether it can actually take a booking — a checkout, cart, calendar or payment step — or whether it sends every booking to WhatsApp, phone or email. Four sites that had gone dead were reclassified as having no website.
5
Classification. Operators reachable only via WhatsApp, Instagram, TikTok, Linktree, a Google Business Profile, or third-party marketplaces were classified as having no website of their own. "Can take an online booking" means a booking can be completed on a channel the operator owns.

Scope & limits. This is a hand-audited convenience sample, not a random-probability sample, weighted toward Malaysia's main tourism hubs (which is where the operators are). The website figure is directional and, at n = 252 across 14 activity types, has stayed within the 72–74% band across successive sampling rounds. The 8% bookable figure comes from an operator-by-operator live check of all 66 website-owning operators, not an estimate.

Why it matters

Good operators, no channel of their own.

The problem is not the operator's rating or the demand for their tour. It is that they don't own the way guests reach them. An operator on WhatsApp and Instagram borrows their audience from those platforms. They pay for it in one of two ways: a commission to a marketplace, or the hours it takes to answer "is this available?" by hand, every day.

A website often does not fix this. Among the 26% who have a live site, 70% still send every booking to phone or WhatsApp. One example: a Perhentian dive resort whose site lists the packages but asks you to call or email, with online payment available only on a marketplace. For context, Malaysia's Ministry of Tourism lists 5,284 licensed travel agencies. Almost all of them are ticketing and inbound-package agencies. The ATV parks, dive centres and island boats that visitors actually book are mostly not on that list, and not on the open web.

This is the gap Kong works on. We give Southeast Asian operators a booking channel they own. It is free for the operator. Guests pay a small booking fee at checkout. A five-star experience can take a booking without a five-message WhatsApp thread.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

What percentage of Malaysian tour operators can take an online booking?
Only 8%. In a 2026 hand-audit of 252 Malaysian tour, activity and experience operators by Kong, just 8% could take a booking online on a channel they own. The other 92% relied on WhatsApp, Instagram, walk-ins, phone, email, or a third-party marketplace.
How many Malaysian tour operators have their own website?
About 26%. In the same study, 74% of operators had no website of their own — they were reachable only through social media, messaging apps, a Google Business Profile, or a marketplace listing.
If an operator has a website, can they take a booking on it?
Usually not. Of the 66 operators in the study that had a working website, only 20 — about 30% — could actually complete a booking online. The other 70% had a website that showed packages and prices but still routed every booking to WhatsApp, phone or email.
Which types of tour operators are least likely to be online?
Owner-run, spontaneous-experience categories. Every horse-riding and escape-room operator in the sample had no website of their own. Watersports, go-karts, paintball and craft workshops were 80% or more offline. Diving, cooking schools and white-water rafting had the most websites, but even in those categories roughly half the operators had none.
How was this study conducted?
Kong built a sample of 252 independent operators by searching Google Maps across 14 Malaysian regions and 14 activity types. Each operator was checked by hand for a website of their own, verified against Semrush domain-indexation data. All 66 operators with a live website were then visited to confirm whether a booking could be completed online, or only an enquiry sent.
