The State of Tour Operator Bookability in Southeast Asia

We checked whether 2,415 tour and activity operators across six countries can actually take a booking online. Most cannot.

Scope: this report measures digitisation through a single question: can a traveller complete a booking from a screen? It does not measure back-office systems, channel management, accounting or social media use. Where it says an operator is not bookable, that is a statement about one capability, not about the business.

2,334 operators classified 43 destinations 31,347 pages crawled Published 25 August 2026

By Blake Ng, Kong · Data released under CC BY 4.0

Headline

Less than 1 out of 8 operators are bookable online

A customer who finds a Southeast Asian tour operator on Google can book directly on the operator's website only 11.8% of the time. The other 88% end in a contact form, a WhatsApp number, a phone number, or nothing at all.

Being online is not the same as being bookable. Vietnam has the second-highest online presence in the region (86.6%) but the third-lowest bookability (9.8%), 2 out of 3 Vietnamese operators built a website that cannot take a booking. Malaysia has the opposite problem: 40.1% have no web presence at all, the highest in the region.

Finding 02

Most bookable operators run shopping carts, not booking software

Of the 275 operators where customers can book directly, only 71 run proper reservation software. The rest sell tours through a general shopping cart (mostly WooCommerce) which can sell a product with a price, but cannot handle availability, capacity or departure times.

Counted against all operators, not just the bookable ones, dedicated booking software reaches only 3.0% of the region, 12.6% in Singapore, 7.2% in Thailand, 3.2% in Malaysia, and under 1.5% in Vietnam, Indonesia and the Philippines.

Finding 03

WhatsApp is the region's booking system

485 operators have a working website whose only route to a reservation is a WhatsApp link. For every 1 operator who can take a booking online, there are 1.8 operators who can only be booked over WhatsApp.

It is not evenly spread. Among Indonesian operators who have a website but no checkout, 64.1% route booking through WhatsApp. In Vietnam it is 45.1%, in Malaysia 19.0%.

Why it matters

These operators have not failed to adopt technology. They adopted a different one. Any study that only counts checkouts and ignores WhatsApp will make this region look further behind than it really is, and any product that treats WhatsApp as the competition has misread the market.

Finding 04

Operators with no website are rarely on OTAs either

556 operators have no web presence of any kind. For them, a listing on an online travel agency (OTA) such as Viator, GetYourGuide or Klook is the only way to be booked from a screen. We checked all 431 of them in Indonesia, Vietnam and the Philippines, one by one.

9 out of 10 have no OTA listing either. No website, no Viator, no GetYourGuide, no Klook. Businesses with hundreds of Google reviews that cannot be booked online anywhere.

The 37 who are listed share one thing: the operator's own name is the brand: a named cooking class, a named guide, a named canyoneering outfit. In the commodity categories, almost nothing is listed. Cebu canyoneering, El Nido island hopping, Boracay watersports, Bohol island tours, Siargao surf schools and dive centres across Indonesia and the Philippines returned almost nothing, even though OTAs carry deep inventory for all of them. That inventory belongs to middlemen and resellers, not to the operators running the boats.

Around 40 of the operators recorded as unlisted do have a TripAdvisor review page with no bookable product attached. They have presence without distribution. The same applies to PADI and DiveSSI dive-centre directory profiles.

Finding 05

Only 38 of 275 bookable operators accept local payments

Of the 275 operators where customers can book directly, 38 accept a local payment method, QRIS or GoPay in Indonesia, PromptPay in Thailand, FPX or DuitNow in Malaysia, MoMo or VNPay in Vietnam, GCash in the Philippines. The rest are card-only, which in practice means selling to foreigners.

Vietnam is the sharpest case: 2 of 41 bookable operators take a Vietnamese payment method. Thailand, with the second-highest bookability in the region, manages 6 of 88.

This is where the ranking flips. Malaysia has half Singapore's bookability but more than double its local-payment rate, 11 of 51 against 4 of 27. Being bookable and accepting local payment are two different things, and a single combined score would hide that.

Finding 06

Diving is the least bookable category in the region

Diving is the least bookable category in the region, even though it costs the most to run and is the product travellers most often book in advance.

Method

How this was measured

Sourcing

Operators were sampled from Google Business Profiles across 43 destinations in six countries, using English-language category searches, capped at 500 per country. Singapore is a census of 98 rather than a sample. Businesses were included if they sell a tour, activity or experience; accommodation, restaurants, retail and equipment rental were excluded.

Classification

Each operator's own domain was crawled to a depth of one page from the homepage (31,347 pages in total) and the HTML fingerprinted for booking platforms, payment gateways, cart software and date-selection widgets. An operator counts as bookable when a date widget appears alongside a booking platform, a payment gateway or cart software on the same site.

Why depth matters

Booking widgets usually live on product pages, not homepages. An earlier homepage-only pass understated Singapore's bookability by a factor of 5.7. Every figure here comes from the depth-1 crawl, and all six countries were re-crawled on one identical pipeline so that no country is measured by an older method.

Verification

The crawler does not execute JavaScript, so 36 operators were re-crawled with a full browser to test whether client-side booking widgets were being missed. None were: zero booking platforms were found that the raw-HTTP crawl had not already detected. Six known-bookable operators were included as controls and reproduced.

Limits

What this does not measure, and where it is soft

Every figure above should be read with these in view. They are stated here rather than in a footnote because several of them materially affect the numbers.

55% of the bookable count rests on softer evidence

150 of the 275 bookable operators qualify through cart software plus a date widget rather than a named booking platform or payment gateway. WooCommerce in particular fingerprints on CSS shipped with a theme, so a site can match without a working shop, and the test cannot separate a real checkout from an enquiry form that happens to carry a date field. Every one of these rows is tagged in the dataset, so the stricter definition can be recovered with a filter: it yields 125 bookable operators, or 5.4%.

81 operators could not be classified

Their sites returned HTTP 403, 429, 502, 522 or TLS errors on repeated attempts from both datacentre and residential proxies. Crawler statistics show zero DNS failures across all of them, so these are blocked or misconfigured rather than dead. They are excluded from every denominator rather than counted as absent, which would have flattered the presence figures.

The OTA finding covers three countries, not six

Indonesia, Vietnam and the Philippines were measured completely, all 431 website-less operators, one search each, one documented evidence standard requiring a bookable product page with a price and date selection. Malaysia, Thailand and Singapore were measured earlier under a different and less rigorous process and are excluded. Singapore's rows in particular carry a blanket negative with no per-row evidence, so they are not comparable and are not published.

No independent accuracy audit was run

A hand-verification pass over a random sample was planned and cut. There is therefore no published accuracy or error rate for the classification. The JavaScript verification described above tests one specific failure mode (missed client-side widgets) and does not substitute for a general audit.

Category mix is not standardised across countries

The Philippines sample is 37.3% dive operators against Thailand's 10.6%. Since diving is the least bookable category, part of the difference between national figures reflects what each country sells rather than how well it sells it. The per-category table is provided so this can be adjusted for.

It measures capability, not usage

A working checkout is not evidence that anyone uses it, and its absence is not evidence that an operator is failing. Many of these businesses are fully booked through WhatsApp, walk-ups and hotel desks. This is a measure of what a traveller can do from a screen, nothing more.

Publisher's interest

Kong sells booking software to tour operators, which gives us an interest in the finding that most of them lack it. Kong's own software was detected on 5 of the 275 bookable operators, 1.8%, all in Malaysia. The underlying data is published in full so that anyone can check the numbers against their own reading of them.

Questions

Common questions

What share of Southeast Asian tour operators can be booked online?

11.8%. Of 2,334 tour and activity operators classified across Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia, Vietnam, the Philippines and Indonesia, 275 let customers book directly on their website. The remainder route to a contact form, a messaging app, a phone number, or have no web presence at all.

Which Southeast Asian country has the highest online bookability?

Singapore, at 28.4% of classified operators, followed by Thailand at 17.6%. Malaysia is 10.2%, Vietnam 9.8%, the Philippines 8.4% and Indonesia 8.1%. Singapore is measured as a census of its market rather than a sample.

How many tour operators in Indonesia accept online bookings?

8.1%: 37 of 456 classified Indonesian operators. A further 274 have a website that cannot complete a booking, and 104 have no web presence at all. Among Indonesian operators with a website but no checkout, 64.1% route bookings through WhatsApp.

What booking software do Southeast Asian tour operators use?

Most bookable operators use a general shopping cart rather than reservation software. WooCommerce appears on 166 of the 275, followed by Bokun on 35, Stripe on 29 and FareHarbor on 24. Purpose-built booking platforms reach only 3.0% of all operators in the region.

Do Southeast Asian tour operators accept local payment methods?

Rarely. 38 of 275 bookable operators (13.8%) accept a local payment method such as QRIS, PromptPay, FPX, MoMo or GCash. In Vietnam it is 2 of 41. The rest are card-only, which in practice restricts them to selling to international travellers.

How many tour operators have no website at all?

556 of 2,334, or 23.8%. The rate ranges from 7.4% in Singapore to 40.1% in Malaysia. A further 286 operators have only a social media profile.

Can tour operators without a website be booked through an OTA?

Usually not. Of 431 website-less operators checked individually in Indonesia, Vietnam and the Philippines, 37 (8.6%) had a bookable listing on an online travel agency. Roughly 9 out of 10 had neither a website nor an OTA listing, meaning they cannot be booked online anywhere.

How was the State of Tour Operator Bookability in Southeast Asia measured?

Operators were sampled from Google Business Profiles across 43 destinations, then each operator's own website was crawled one level deep (31,347 pages) and fingerprinted for booking platforms, payment gateways, cart software and date-selection widgets. An operator counts as bookable when a date widget appears alongside a booking platform, gateway or cart. A 36-operator browser-rendered control confirmed the crawler was not missing JavaScript-injected booking widgets.
